# Signals Roadmap

This document turns the FTFN signal strategy into an actionable build queue.

It should be used when deciding which signals to research, draft, repair, review, or promote next. It does not replace the editorial method, source strategy, review checklists, publication policy, or source verification process.

Core framing:

```text
42 is possibility.
59 is urgency.
Civilization is a choice.

The future is not a list of inventions.
It is a stack of dependencies.
```

## Purpose

FTFN should not chase every interesting announcement. A high-value signal should help readers understand one of four things:

- a frontier capability is becoming more real,
- a dependency is becoming more binding,
- a constraint is tightening or relaxing,
- a local system is converting, delaying, distorting, or rejecting a global signal.

The signal roadmap is the bridge between broad content ambition and publishable editorial work. It helps answer:

```text
What should we watch next, and why does it matter?
```

## Current Baseline

As of the Phase 37 source monitor:

- FTFN has a working static Astro site prepared for `ftfn.io`.
- The signal model, source model, topic atlas, evidence gaps, local systems, dependency maps, and publication policy exist.

This roadmap should shape the next content expansion cycle, using the source monitor as the first filter for source currency and authority.

## What Makes A Signal High Value

A signal is high value when it scores well qualitatively across these lenses:

### Dependency Relevance

Does the signal touch a dependency that many future systems rely on?

Examples:

- power availability,
- grid interconnection,
- water,
- critical minerals,
- advanced chips,
- standards,
- airspace rules,
- launch capacity,
- local permitting,
- insurance and financing,
- workforce.

### Conversion Evidence

Does the signal show movement from information into outcomes?

Examples:

- policy to implementation,
- award to construction,
- prototype to certification,
- standard to migration,
- forecast to operational response,
- permit to completion,
- source discovery to processing capacity.

### Constraint Visibility

Does the signal reveal a limit, bottleneck, missing input, or tradeoff?

Examples:

- transmission queue delays,
- transformer supply constraints,
- water-supply requirements,
- local housing or workforce pressure,
- regulatory readiness,
- climate volatility,
- cybersecurity transition timelines.

### Local System Fit

Can the signal be interpreted through a receiving system?

Examples:

- Ontario housing supply and real estate,
- U.S. Southwest chip corridor,
- airport and airspace systems,
- utility service territories,
- agricultural regions,
- coastal or drought-prone climate regions.

### Source Strength

Can the signal be supported by official, primary, or highly credible sources?

Preferred source anchors:

- government agencies,
- regulators,
- public data portals,
- standards bodies,
- research labs,
- universities and publicly funded research organizations,
- utility/regulatory filings,
- public company filings,
- company press rooms only when clearly labeled as interested-party evidence,
- credible reporting used mainly for context, not unsupported claims.

## Watch Lanes

These watch lanes should become the recurring editorial spine of FTFN signals. They can later become public filters, newsletter sections, briefing categories, or Atlas entry points.

### Power Watch

Focus:

- AI and data center electricity demand,
- grid planning,
- load growth,
- power availability for industrial strategy,
- transmission and interconnection,
- transformer and electrical-equipment supply.

Why it matters:

Power is becoming a binding dependency for AI, chips, electrification, advanced manufacturing, water infrastructure, mining, and local economic development.

Signal examples:

- an official load-growth forecast changes materially,
- a regional reliability assessment flags resource adequacy risk,
- a transmission project enters service or is delayed,
- an interconnection reform changes queue conversion prospects,
- electrical equipment supply becomes a documented constraint.

### Compute And Chips Watch

Focus:

- semiconductor manufacturing,
- advanced packaging,
- lithography and process nodes,
- CHIPS Act or equivalent industrial policy,
- chip supply chains,
- compute bottlenecks for AI, autonomy, quantum, defense, and science.

Why it matters:

Chips are an enabling layer for nearly every frontier domain. The key signal is not only a funding announcement; it is whether money becomes fabs, equipment, workforce, water, power, and yield.

Signal examples:

- award-to-construction conversion,
- fab opening, delay, or scope change,
- advanced packaging capacity milestone,
- export control or supply-chain shift,
- workforce or equipment constraint.

### Minerals And Materials Watch

Focus:

- critical minerals,
- mine-to-refining pathways,
- processing and separation capacity,
- recycling,
- substitution,
- AI for chemistry and materials science.

Why it matters:

Future systems need physical inputs. Discovery is not enough; extraction, processing, permitting, transport, refining, and manufacturing conversion all matter.

Signal examples:

- official mineral commodity update,
- new mine permit milestone,
- processing plant financing or commissioning,
- strategic reserve or trade policy change,
- materials breakthrough with a credible path to scale.

### Water Watch

Focus:

- industrial water demand,
- water rights and supply rules,
- drought,
- groundwater and basin management,
- desalination or reuse,
- water as a local constraint on advanced manufacturing, housing, agriculture, and energy.

Why it matters:

Water is often invisible until it becomes a veto point. FTFN should treat water as a core receiving-system constraint, especially in arid industrial corridors.

Signal examples:

- water-supply approval or denial,
- basin management threshold,
- drought status shift,
- utility or municipal servicing constraint,
- facility-level conservation or reuse commitment with official support.

### Mobility Certification Watch

Focus:

- eVTOL and advanced air mobility,
- autonomous vehicles,
- drones and BVLOS rules,
- airspace integration,
- safety reporting,
- infrastructure readiness.

Why it matters:

Transportation futures are constrained by certification, safety, insurance, public acceptance, infrastructure, and operating domain limits as much as by vehicle performance.

Signal examples:

- FAA certification milestone,
- AAM implementation plan update,
- vertiport or airport integration decision,
- NHTSA automated-vehicle reporting update,
- drone BVLOS rule or waiver expansion.

### Climate Conversion Watch

Focus:

- ENSO,
- drought,
- heat,
- flood risk,
- climate services,
- infrastructure adaptation,
- how forecasts become operational choices.

Why it matters:

Climate information matters only when institutions convert it into water, grid, agriculture, insurance, planning, emergency management, or investment decisions.

Signal examples:

- NOAA ENSO outlook shift,
- seasonal drought outlook change,
- climate-risk guidance from a public agency,
- grid or water operator response to forecast conditions,
- insurance or lending response to physical risk.

### Agriculture And Bioeconomy Watch

Focus:

- gene editing,
- plant genomics,
- bioinformatics,
- climate-resilient crops,
- precision agriculture,
- robotics and autonomy,
- soil, water, and input efficiency.

Why it matters:

Agriculture is where climate, biology, data, energy, water, trade, and local systems converge.

Signal examples:

- USDA or equivalent program update,
- gene-edited crop regulatory milestone,
- public genomics dataset or research infrastructure,
- climate-resilience trial result,
- precision-agriculture deployment with measurable constraints.

### AI For Science And Materials Watch

Focus:

- AI in chemistry,
- materials discovery,
- digital twins,
- laboratory automation,
- scientific datasets,
- research-to-manufacturing conversion.

Why it matters:

AI may accelerate discovery, but FTFN should watch the conversion layer: validation, reproducibility, manufacturability, regulation, supply chains, and cost.

Signal examples:

- public research program milestone,
- standards or dataset release,
- lab-to-pilot manufacturing step,
- validated materials result with manufacturing relevance,
- failed or delayed scale-up that reveals constraints.

### Space Infrastructure Watch

Focus:

- launch cadence,
- commercial space regulation,
- Artemis and lunar infrastructure,
- satellite networks,
- in-space manufacturing,
- space logistics,
- theoretical futures only when clearly separated from current evidence.

Why it matters:

Space is both frontier possibility and dependency stack: launch capacity, funding, mission reliability, regulation, energy, communications, materials, and geopolitics.

Signal examples:

- FAA commercial launch licensing update,
- NASA Artemis infrastructure milestone or delay,
- launch-site capacity change,
- satellite constellation regulatory decision,
- space logistics demonstration.

### Security And Standards Watch

Focus:

- post-quantum cryptography,
- critical infrastructure cybersecurity,
- AI safety and governance standards,
- interoperability,
- standards-to-deployment conversion.

Why it matters:

Standards are often the hinge between invention and system adoption. The critical question is whether organizations can actually migrate.

Signal examples:

- NIST standard publication,
- agency migration guidance,
- procurement requirement,
- sector-level implementation deadline,
- evidence of institutional readiness or delay.

### Finance, Insurance, And Workforce Watch

Focus:

- project finance,
- insurance,
- capital costs,
- labor availability,
- training pipelines,
- local fiscal capacity,
- public procurement.

Why it matters:

Many futures fail at the economic and institutional layer, not the technical layer.

Signal examples:

- financing close for a major infrastructure project,
- insurance restriction or new underwriting posture,
- workforce training program tied to a real project,
- cost escalation in a critical supply chain,
- public procurement or budget decision.

### Local Systems Watch

Focus:

- how global signals are received by specific places, sectors, regulators, utilities, housing markets, water systems, and labor markets.

Why it matters:

FTFN's most distinctive analytical edge is contextual transduction: understanding how information, capital, technology, and policy become outcomes inside local constraint environments.

Signal examples:

- a facility-specific utility filing,
- a municipal permit or planning decision,
- a water-supply approval,
- a housing-starts gap in a high-growth region,
- a local workforce bottleneck tied to an industrial strategy.

## Evidence Requirements By Lane

### Power, Grid, And Equipment

Minimum evidence:

- official load, reliability, planning, or procurement source,
- date-specific change,
- clear distinction between forecast, plan, approval, and built capacity.

Avoid:

- treating projected demand as guaranteed demand,
- treating planned generation or transmission as delivered capacity.

### Chips And Advanced Manufacturing

Minimum evidence:

- official program award, filing, permit, construction, workforce, power, water, or commissioning evidence,
- clear relationship between the signal and a dependency stack.

Avoid:

- treating a press release as proof of local readiness,
- treating national industrial policy as facility-level conversion.

### Minerals, Materials, And AI For Science

Minimum evidence:

- official commodity, permitting, research, or program source,
- distinction between discovery, demonstration, pilot, commercial scale, and system adoption.

Avoid:

- treating breakthrough claims as manufacturing readiness,
- ignoring processing, refining, or substitution constraints.

### Mobility

Minimum evidence:

- regulator, safety agency, airport authority, infrastructure operator, or official certification source,
- clear deployment boundary.

Avoid:

- treating test flights, concept vehicles, or company claims as deployment.

### Climate And Agriculture

Minimum evidence:

- official forecast, monitoring, research, regulatory, or program source,
- clear time horizon and region,
- explicit caveats.

Avoid:

- making deterministic local outcome claims from broad climate outlooks,
- treating research promise as near-term adoption.

### Local Systems

Minimum evidence:

- local official source, regulator, utility, planning document, permit, application, provider record, or public dataset,
- explicit statement of what the evidence does not prove.

Avoid:

- broad local conclusions without local evidence,
- facility-level claims from regional-only data.

## Suggested Record Sequence

When building the next signal batch, use this sequence:

1. Add or verify source records first.
2. Check `/atlas/source-monitor/` for source freshness and review priority.
3. Add missing organization or technology records only when needed for cross-links.
4. Repair existing related signals before creating new broad records.
5. Create new signal records only when a specific dated event, dataset, filing, rule, report, or milestone exists.
6. Keep new records in `Draft` or `In Review` until they pass review.
7. Update evidence gaps when a signal reveals missing conversion evidence.
8. Update local system profiles only when local evidence supports the relationship.
9. Promote to `Published` only through the publication policy and launch-candidate review path.

## Reader-Facing Experience Direction

The signals should eventually feel less like a chronological feed and more like an intelligence dashboard.

Near-term public surfaces:

- Published-first signal index.
- Review shelf for transparent prelaunch records.
- Lane labels such as Power Watch, Water Watch, Mobility Certification Watch, and Climate Conversion Watch.
- Clear editorial state, verification state, evidence quality, and source cards.

Later public surfaces:

- watch-lane filters,
- local-system filters,
- dependency-stack filters,
- "signals to watch next" modules on local system and topic pages,
- briefings built from reviewed signal clusters,
- qualitative 42/59 labels only after enough reviewed records exist.

Do not add numeric scoring until the qualitative signal base is mature enough to justify a rubric.

## Recommended Next Content Phase After Launch QA

After Phase 37 is complete, the next content phase should be a focused source-backed signal-lane expansion, not a broad content dump.

Recommended scope:

- use `/atlas/source-monitor/` to identify review-due sources before drafting new claims,
- choose 6 to 8 candidate signals from the highest-value batch,
- add only the source records needed for those signals,
- prioritize Power Watch, Compute and Chips Watch, Water Watch, Mobility Certification Watch, Agriculture and Bioeconomy Watch, AI for Science and Materials Watch, and Space Infrastructure Watch,
- repair existing CHIPS, FAA, NHTSA, NASA, and USDA records where possible,
- create new records only where official source evidence supports a specific signal,
- keep local-system conclusions cautious,
- promote no additional records to `Published` unless the separate publication gate is explicitly invoked.

Suggested acceptance criteria:

- new or repaired signals are specific, dated, sourced, and evidence-aware,
- every signal has a clear dependency-stack reason to exist,
- every local implication has a source-backed boundary,
- evidence gaps are added or updated where conversion evidence is missing,
- validation, check, and build pass if app/content files change.
