# Glossary

This glossary defines the working language of Forty Two Fifty Nine. It should stay practical: terms exist so the project can be built, not so the project can admire its own vocabulary.

## 42/59 Index

A future branded assessment system balancing possibility and urgency.

- 42: transformative potential, possibility, enabling power.
- 59: urgency, fragility, constraint pressure, time sensitivity.

MVP status:

- Qualitative only.
- Avoid false precision.

Possible later use:

- Signal labels.
- Topic dashboards.
- Briefing sections.
- Research reports.

## Briefing

A recurring editorial synthesis of important signals.

Briefings should explain:

- what changed,
- which dependencies moved,
- which constraints tightened or loosened,
- which stories are noise,
- what to watch next.

## Constraint

A condition that can slow, block, distort, or shape a future outcome.

Examples:

- power,
- materials,
- water,
- compute,
- manufacturing,
- regulation,
- certification,
- capital,
- insurance,
- cybersecurity,
- labor,
- public trust,
- climate,
- geopolitics,
- unit economics,
- supply chain,
- standards.

## Constraint Map

A structured view of the constraints that affect a technology, place, project, organization, or signal.

Use it to ask:

```text
What has to be true for this to scale?
What could stop it?
What bottlenecks are local, sectoral, or global?
```

## Contextual Transduction

The process by which a system converts external signals into locally meaningful decisions and outcomes.

The core model:

```text
Signal + Receiving System + Constraint Map
```

This is a theoretical lens that may become a product feature through local system profiles, signal-to-system impact analysis, and place-based briefings.

## Dependency Stack

The layered set of conditions required for a technology, policy, project, or future state to become real.

Example:

```text
Autonomous aviation depends on batteries, sensors, software, certification, airspace systems, manufacturing, insurance, weather operations, infrastructure, and public trust.
```

## Enabling Infrastructure

The systems that make frontier domains deployable.

Examples:

- energy and grid,
- chips and semiconductor manufacturing,
- data centers,
- AI infrastructure,
- quantum systems,
- communications,
- cybersecurity,
- advanced manufacturing,
- standards and certification,
- finance and insurance.

## Evidence Quality

An editorial judgment about how well a claim is supported.

MVP levels:

- Primary source
- Official data
- Peer-reviewed research
- Credible reporting
- Credible analysis
- Company claim
- Unverified claim
- Speculative claim

Evidence quality should never be used to make weak evidence sound stronger than it is.

## Frontier Domain

A visible technology or capability frontier.

Examples:

- autonomous vehicles,
- drones,
- eVTOL,
- aviation,
- robotics,
- space travel,
- lunar infrastructure,
- LiDAR archaeology,
- underwater exploration,
- agriculture genetics,
- bioinformatics,
- synthetic biology,
- AI for chemistry and materials science.

## FTFN

The working shorthand for Forty Two Fifty Nine.

Use:

- in internal docs,
- in work packages,
- in implementation notes,
- in project conversations.

Preserve the full name, Forty Two Fifty Nine, where the brand needs to be introduced or where public-facing context benefits from the full phrase.

## Human System

The social, institutional, economic, political, and cultural systems that determine whether change is adopted, rejected, delayed, financed, governed, or trusted.

Examples:

- policy,
- labor,
- education,
- public trust,
- safety,
- ethics,
- accessibility,
- geopolitics,
- defense and dual-use technology,
- inequality,
- cultural acceptance.

## Informational Velocity

The speed and fidelity with which information moves through a system and becomes action.

This is theoretical. It should be used carefully because speed is not always good.

Related distinctions:

- informational velocity,
- sensemaking quality,
- decision velocity,
- implementation velocity,
- learning velocity,
- legitimacy.

## Local Equilibrium

The current balance of forces in a place, sector, institution, or market.

Examples of forces:

- power supply,
- water availability,
- housing,
- labor,
- regulation,
- capital,
- land use,
- political coalitions,
- infrastructure,
- climate exposure.

A signal can disturb a local equilibrium by changing incentives, bottlenecks, opportunities, or risks.

## Local System Profile

A structured profile of a place, sector, institution, or market as a receiving system for frontier signals.

It should describe:

- current equilibrium,
- key industries,
- energy and water constraints,
- infrastructure,
- labor,
- regulation,
- capital flows,
- climate exposure,
- major projects,
- relevant signals,
- likely second-order effects.

## Maturity Ladder

A controlled vocabulary for judging how real or deployable a signal, technology, or project is.

MVP levels:

```text
Theory
Lab Result
Prototype
Field Trial
Pilot Program
Early Commercial
Scaling
Infrastructure
Commodity
Decline or Failure
```

## Planetary Condition

An environmental baseline that shapes the future-state system.

Examples:

- climate systems,
- ENSO,
- oceans,
- water,
- drought,
- flood,
- heat,
- wildfire,
- food security,
- habitability,
- migration.

## Receiving System

The place, sector, institution, market, or organization that receives a signal and may convert it into local meaning, decisions, or outcomes.

Examples:

- Ontario real estate,
- Arizona semiconductor manufacturing,
- Alberta energy systems,
- Chilean lithium supply chains,
- Gulf Coast ports,
- North American aviation regulators.

## Resource Foundation

The raw physical inputs and supply chains needed for future systems.

Examples:

- geology,
- critical minerals,
- mining,
- refining,
- recycling,
- battery materials,
- rare earths,
- semiconductor materials,
- industrial water,
- land use,
- supply-chain chokepoints.

## Signal

The basic editorial object of Forty Two Fifty Nine.

A signal is a structured summary of a development that may affect future-state systems.

Signals can include:

- press releases,
- research results,
- launches,
- regulations,
- accidents,
- funding rounds,
- climate updates,
- company milestones,
- reports,
- datasets,
- market shifts.

Every signal should identify:

- what changed,
- who claims it,
- why it matters,
- dependencies,
- constraints,
- maturity,
- time horizon,
- evidence quality,
- source.

## Source

An origin point for information.

Source types include:

- government agency,
- company press room,
- research lab,
- university,
- standards body,
- international organization,
- dataset,
- credible reporting,
- trade publication.

## Source Credibility

A working classification of how directly and reliably a source supports a claim.

Credibility levels are defined in [Source Strategy](source-strategy.md).

## Technology

A method, tool, system, platform, or technical capability that can be tracked across signals, projects, organizations, and topics.

Examples:

- LiDAR,
- eVTOL,
- sodium-ion batteries,
- photonic quantum computing,
- direct air capture,
- autonomous navigation,
- CRISPR crop editing.

## Time Horizon

An editorial classification of when a signal or capability may matter.

MVP levels:

```text
Now
2-5 Years
5-15 Years
15+ Years
Speculative
```

## Transduction

The conversion of information into material, institutional, technical, economic, or behavioral outcomes.

In physical systems, transduction converts signals across media. In social systems, it converts observed reality into shared knowledge, decisions, resource movement, and action.

This is theoretical, but important to the long-term analytical identity of the project.
