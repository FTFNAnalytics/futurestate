# FTFN next-build roadmap — Phases 205–216

**Planning baseline:** September 9, 2026.  
**Status:** Historical September 9 proposal; starting-state and scheduling assumptions superseded by the [September 14 revision](roadmap-next-phases-205-216-2026-09-14.md).  
**Basis:** [Build and content audit](audits/2026-09-09-build-and-content-audit.md).  
**Planning horizon:** Approximately 8–12 weeks after work begins, subject to actual evidence returns and reviewer availability. Dates below are targets, not completed events or promised approvals.

FTFN's next build should make the existing knowledge base easier to trust, understand, and use. The program turns the strongest existing material into a substantive public reference, establishes a working evidence-to-publication cycle, and makes releases reproducible. It preserves the five-layer dependency thesis and the seventeen-topic framework.

**September 14 checkpoint:** Local reader-text and current-evidence additions now provide foundations for Phases 205, 207, 208, 212 and 213. Their full current build validation is pending; neither those additions nor this planning revision certify phase acceptance. Use the revised roadmap for current priorities and dates. The findings and schedule below retain their September 9 context.

Phases 181–204 remain implemented historical content architecture. This roadmap supersedes their generic “next steps” for execution priority, while retaining their receipts, evidence restrictions, scheduled operations, and owner-controlled decisions. It does not mark them factually complete, alter their recorded gates, or authorize deployment.

## The product outcome

At the end of this program, a reader should be able to:

1. Get a direct, sourced answer to a priority question about a frontier system.
2. Follow that answer into a named project or place, with a dated stage and visible limitations.
3. Distinguish observed facts, issuer claims, FTFN interpretation, method notes, and unanswered research.
4. Inspect a small set of compatible observations where the evidence supports them.
5. See what changed, when it was checked, and why a correction was made.
6. Use the reference on a phone, with a keyboard or assistive technology, and on a constrained connection.

The release should be measured by those outcomes. Route counts, phase counts, test-fixture counts, source links, and unfilled packets remain implementation measures.

## Content priorities

### First: repair the meaning of existing content

Review the raw 1,406 signals before treating them as 1,406 external developments. Separate method/test notes from externally observed events and from source-reported claims. Do the same for the launch-selected briefings and research records. Recalculate topic and homepage counts from the correct content types.

Preserve the 285 In Review signals as a research queue, with terminal dispositions such as accepted, corrected, narrowed, merged with lineage, held pending an exact artifact, or retired with a reason. Start with a manually selected batch of 25 based on reader value, relevance to scheduled gates, and evidence availability. A disposition is the target; publication is not a quota. Reuse Phase 182's full queue and first-pass cohort rather than introducing another backlog identity system.

### Second: complete four thin-topic editions

| Topic | Initial reader question | Required evidence mix | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Climate | Which changing physical conditions constrain named infrastructure and operating systems? | Primary observation/data, methodology, local receiving-system evidence | Topic explainer, two bounded cases, constraints and uncertainty |
| Agriculture and Bioeconomy | Which capabilities have moved from laboratory results into measured field or production use? | Primary research, trial/operating evidence, authority where relevant | Research-to-use account, two cases, limits on transfer |
| Quantum | What has been demonstrated, under what benchmark, and what still prevents useful deployment? | Primary paper/technical result, disclosed benchmark, independent or authority context where available | Capability/constraint explainer, two cases, benchmark caveats |
| Discovery Technologies | What do sensing and exploration tools establish, and what remains interpretation? | Primary method/data, independent validation where available, provenance | Tool-to-finding account, two cases, confidence limits |

Each edition needs at least three distinct exact primary artifacts, including one with substantive methodology or measured evidence. A portal may aid discovery but does not satisfy that requirement. Aim for two useful cases per topic; hold unsupported claims rather than filling gaps with generic copy. Add new signals only for distinct external developments that improve these editions.

### Third: deepen named projects and places

Use Phase 119's 24 projects and 15 places as the identity backbone, crosswalk the four exact Phase 191 anchors, and resolve selected existing acquisition dockets. The initial global tranche targets eight projects and four places across at least four jurisdictions and three world regions. At least half the project tranche should be outside the United States; native-language sources require reliable interpretation.

Each project gets a stable identity, location, responsible entity, dated stage, exact supporting artifacts, chronology, constraints, and the next decisive evidence. Each place explains system capacity and dependencies without inheriting one project's stage. Merge aliases through a crosswalk; never inflate the count by duplicating profiles.

The existing 100-project and 70-place goals remain long-range coverage goals and existing Phase 204 conditions until the owner explicitly revises release scope. Eight/four is the next production tranche, not a claim that the larger gate is satisfied.

### Fourth: publish measured analysis only after admission

Assess three existing pilot candidates for a fixed identity, stable measure, compatible unit/denominator, dated periods, transparent method, and revision lineage. Two observations may demonstrate repeat measurement; they do not by themselves justify a trend. Prefer three or more compatible periods for the first public series and state the analytical limits.

Ship the outcomes feature only if at least one pilot passes actual admission and review. If none qualifies, publish a concise account of what is missing and leave the feature unshipped. Do not manufacture a measurement, convert a national statistic into a project result, or reset a Held decision to make a milestone look complete.

## Technical direction

Keep the static Astro/MDX/JSON architecture while it fits the reading product. Preserve canonical identities and historical snapshots. Add a versioned current-state projection with explicit later evidence, review, correction, and publication events; it should read existing records rather than replicate entire phase registries.

The current code needs security applicability review and a tested dependency update path, then predictable build and release commands. Optimize the largest generated pages and broad indexes before introducing infrastructure complexity. A database, account system, real-time dashboard, autonomous publishing engine, or new backend migration needs a demonstrated workflow requirement; it is not a prerequisite for these phases.

Search should return concise results by topic, question, project, place, and content type. Method/test records should be discoverable as method records and excluded from an external-developments default. Add pagination or progressive loading to large shelves while retaining crawlable links and basic no-JavaScript access.

Performance targets are proposed acceptance budgets: key reader pages at most 150 KB gzip HTML, low-bandwidth editions at most 100 KB transferred for initial HTML/CSS with no required JavaScript, and no more than 50 initially rendered result cards per large shelf. Measure first and document any justified exception. For hosted testing, target LCP at most 2.5 seconds and CLS at most 0.1 under a declared mobile setup; test real interactions and use a field INP target of at most 200 ms. After launch, assess all three at the 75th percentile when sufficient field data exists. These follow [Google's Core Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals); a Lighthouse load alone does not establish INP or field performance. These are goals, not current measured results.

## Release scope and decisions

Two decisions must remain distinct:

- **A bounded English reference release:** a coherent set of reviewed topics, factual answers, named cases, method/corrections pages, and accessible reading surfaces. This is the recommended first delivery scope. Any omission from existing mandatory Phase 204 requirements needs an explicit owner-approved scope revision and a separately named release decision; it does not turn the historic v1 gate into Pass.
- **The full v1 Public Reference Edition:** retains the broader exact-identity targets, calendar operations, evidence/mission decisions, independent review, language approval, repository/delivery evidence, and final owner decision defined in Phase 204, unless explicitly revised by the owner.

Prepare the exact candidate and review packet before seeking final publication approval. The current audit/planning instruction does not approve a future deployment, access change, domain change, external outreach, or reviewer commission. Routine local implementation and review preparation can proceed when the next build work is authorized.

The decision packet should state what is being released, what remains research-only, which claims changed, the candidate digest/source commit, reviewer findings, unresolved risks, access audience, and rollback target. Launch is blocked by misleading evidence labels, unresolved critical defects, failed release checks, or missing mandatory approvals—not merely by an arbitrary number of open research questions outside scope.

## Capacity, dependencies, and stop rules

Plan approximately 20–30 engineering/product days, 25–40 research/editorial days, and 6–10 reviewer days across the program. These are rough planning estimates, with some work overlapping. External review and evidence returns can extend the 8–12-week horizon. If one person does all production work, plan approximately 12–16 weeks and obtain independent review separately. No monetary budget or external commitment is assumed.

Allocate roughly half the production capacity to evidence acquisition and substantive editing, one third to reader experience/reliability, and the remainder to operations and review preparation. Maintain a weekly limit of 25 active research dispositions and three deeply edited cases; finish or explicitly defer them before opening another batch.

Stop expansion when the selected cohort lacks usable evidence, the editorial queue exceeds capacity, existing critical corrections remain unresolved, or release validation fails. Continue unaffected local work while waiting for external review. Do not predate September/October operations, rewrite historic receipts, fabricate named reviewers, relax an evidence test to meet a count, or call a prepared contract a completed edition.

Then consider additional regional reporting, reviewed French/Spanish editions, more compatible outcome series, and richer data access. A new feature should solve a demonstrated reader problem and have a maintenance owner before it receives a phase number.
