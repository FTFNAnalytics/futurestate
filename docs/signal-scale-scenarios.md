# Signal Scale Scenarios

Date: 2026-07-21

This document maps what FTFN looks like at two signal-library sizes:

- 25 to 35 total signals with 8 to 12 Published or publication-ready candidates,
- 70 total signals with 24 Published or publication-ready candidates.

The point is not volume for its own sake. The point is enough dated, source-backed signal coverage for FTFN to feel like an analytical resource instead of a promising framework.

Current signal library:

Current primary-topic coverage exists for many pillars, but the signal layer is still thin or missing in:

- deeper local evidence for Arizona and Ontario.

## Scenario A: 25 To 35 Signals, 8 To 12 Candidates

This is the v0.2-sized corpus.

### What It Should Feel Like

At this size, FTFN should feel like a serious prelaunch or early-launch intelligence product. A reader should be able to see:

- every active topic has at least one signal,
- the strongest watch lanes have several dated records,
- source freshness is visible,
- local systems have named evidence candidates,
- Published candidates are narrow, sourced, and caveated,
- `In Review` records are clearly research shelf material.

This is not yet a comprehensive watch system. It is the first version where the editorial method starts to prove itself.

### Target Status Mix

| Status | Target Count | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Published | 6 to 8 | Small public evidence core after final review. |
| Publication-ready candidates | 2 to 4 | Records ready for final copy/source/publication check. |
| In Review | 14 to 20 | Research shelf, dated repairs, local constraints, and source-backed frames. |
| Needs Update / Needs Follow-Up | 2 to 4 | Records that are useful but blocked by freshness or specificity. |
| Draft Sample | 0 to 1 | Company-claim examples should be hidden, archived, or heavily de-emphasized. |
| Total | 25 to 35 | Enough breadth to show the system works. |

The 8 to 12 candidate number should include already Published records plus publication-ready records. Do not create 12 published-looking pages if only 3 are truly ready.

### Editorial Gates

Before a record becomes Published or publication-ready:

- source checked date must be current,
- claim must be dated or bounded,
- source limitation must be visible,
- local claims must cite local records,
- company claims must be labeled as interested-party evidence,
- no record should imply full system readiness from a partial source.

## Scenario B: 70 Signals, 24 Candidates

This is the first genuinely comprehensive-resource shape. It is closer to a v0.3 or v0.4 milestone unless v0.2 expands aggressively.

### What It Should Feel Like

At 70 signals, FTFN should feel like a real watch system.

A reader should be able to browse by topic or source lane and find:

- multiple dated records per major topic,
- a clear distinction between Published, candidate, In Review, stale, and blocked records,
- local evidence trails with named records,
- recurring source-update patterns,
- static data exports that make the site useful as a reference layer,
- early signs of cadence: not just what FTFN knows, but how it keeps track.

This is the threshold where stronger UX becomes necessary. Indexes, filters, source coverage summaries, and data exports become product features, not nice-to-haves.

### Target Status Mix

| Status | Target Count | Role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Published | 14 to 18 | Public evidence core across major watch lanes. |
| Publication-ready candidates | 6 to 10 | Final-review shelf for the next public batch. |
| In Review | 32 to 40 | Active research shelf and source-backed records not yet public. |
| Needs Update / Needs Follow-Up | 6 to 10 | Freshness and specificity blockers stay visible. |
| Draft / Archived Samples | 0 to 3 | Samples should not appear as authority. |
| Total | 70 | Broad enough to require real editorial operations. |

The 24-candidate figure should mean:

```text
Published + publication-ready = 24
```

It should not mean 24 records are automatically public.

### Product Requirements At 70

The current page structure can survive 25 to 35 signals. At 70, it needs stronger product support.

Required:

- better signal index filtering by topic, status, evidence quality, and source type,
- stronger source monitor grouping by watch lane and review status,
- signal archive or research shelf separation,
- public update/correction log,
- static JSON exports,
- clearer candidate/publication review surfaces,
- local dossier pages or sections that separate source inventory from named record trails,
- a maintained source-update queue.

Likely needed soon:

- search,
- topic-level latest-signal sections,
- watch-lane pages,
- generated data downloads page,
- editorial calendar or cadence surface,
- stronger browser QA cadence.

Still not needed:

- numeric 42/59 scoring,
- user accounts,
- automated public publishing,
- full CMS/database migration,
- public API guarantees.

## Practical Sequence

### From 18 To 30 Signals

1. Continue the existing private update queue with bounded source-item selection.
2. Add 4 to 6 Phase 50B award, research, local, minerals/trade, or regulatory records.
3. Deepen both local dossiers with 3 to 5 named evidence signals or inputs.
4. Repair 4 to 7 broad In Review records without forcing a count increase.
5. Add Discovery Technologies, AI for Science, and Advanced Manufacturing anchors.
6. Review 8 to 12 records for publication readiness.

### From 30 To 70 Signals

1. Add source batches to reach 110 to 140 sources.
2. Add watch-lane pages or stronger coverage surfaces.
3. Add 35 to 40 new dated signals from official sources.
4. Create static JSON exports.
5. Add public update/correction log.
6. Expand local dossiers from two profiles toward three to five dossiers.
7. Run publication review in batches of 6 to 8 records.

## Recommendation

Use the 25 to 35 signal scenario as the v0.2 target.

Treat the 70 signal scenario as the next major milestone after v0.2, because it requires more than content entry. It requires stronger information architecture, better filtering, static exports, a real update log, and repeatable editorial operations.

The right next move is now:

```text
Bounded source-item selection -> named local evidence -> dated signal repair -> publication review.
```

That is how FTFN gets from a credible scaffold to an authoritative analytical tool without filling the site with undifferentiated content.
