## Complete current reading

Victorian Big Battery provides a clear example of records that describe different moments in an asset's history. The inherited shelf includes commissioning-incident findings, market registration and operational material. Those documents should be read in sequence while keeping the evidence for an incident distinct from evidence about subsequent service.

### Keep the event chronology visible

Energy Safe Victoria's statement, dated September 28, 2021 on its official investigation index, concerns the July 30 commissioning fire involving two Megapacks. It identifies a coolant leak as the most likely initiating fault, with monitoring and protection unavailable in offline service mode contributing to the outcome. That attribution rests on the engineering work, scene examination and telemetry described in the statement, not an independently reproduced investigation here. ESV had no objection to recommencing commissioning provided the specified testing, inspection, monitoring and protection measures were in place. Further fire-spread findings and possible enforcement remained matters for subsequent assessment at that date. Separately, AEMO's October 8 update lists the 300 MW battery among generators registered since July 1. This establishes a registration-reporting window, not an exact registration day or proof of operation on July 1.

### Later performance needs later observations

AEMO's December 8, 2021 announcement reports the System Integrity Protection Scheme reserve service operational following commissioning. It describes a contract for up to 250 MW of reserve, distinct from the Victorian Big Battery facility's 300 MW/450 MWh size. A reserve-service announcement is not a measurement of annual availability, actual dispatch, degradation or customer benefit. The underlying service contract and a compatible subsequent operating ledger were not inspected. The investigation index is likewise a discovery source, not a continuing safety audit. These dated records establish different milestones without proving that every corrective action was closed or that later service was uninterrupted.

### The next decisive account

A stronger profile would connect the commissioning findings to dated verification and subsequent operation using a consistent asset boundary. It would explain changes in configuration and distinguish operator reporting from regulator acceptance. Degradation, revenues and customer benefits would require additional evidence. This structure lets the report describe an incident without assigning permanent failure, and describe later administrative progress without implying universal assurance. The September case remains an organized, source-bound history with current classification and recurring outcomes left for a governed entity-specific review.
