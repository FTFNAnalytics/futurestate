## Complete current reading

Climate exposure, infrastructure design, operating performance and recovery are connected questions with different evidence requirements. A warm year does not identify the cause of an equipment failure, and a record renewable-energy share does not measure an individual battery's reliability. The inherited electricity, climate, launch and battery records make those distinctions visible. This chapter explains how to build a defensible resilience account around named assets, specific events and repeated service rather than around a broad impression of progress.

### Exposure is not the same as failure

NOAA's 2025 assessment describes temperature and extremes using declared regions and historical baselines. It can establish that a period was unusually warm within that frame. It cannot, without further evidence, establish the conditions experienced by a particular asset or attribute an interruption to weather. A resilience account needs the event location, timing, intensity and relevant asset operating conditions.

The next step is to connect exposure to a plausible mechanism and test it against the record. Equipment design, maintenance condition, operating procedures and other disturbances may also matter. These are questions for analysis and source attribution, not reasons to announce a climate cause whenever infrastructure performance changes during an extreme period.

### System composition is not an asset reliability measure

AEMO reports renewable generation at 46.5% of National Electricity Market supply in the first quarter of 2026. It also reports that South Australia's renewable-and-storage contribution reached 98.8% during one half-hour. These are meaningful system and interval observations. They do not state Hornsdale's annual availability, Dalrymple's individual dispatch or the Victorian Big Battery's maintenance condition.

The time windows are central to interpretation. A half-hour maximum cannot substitute for quarterly energy supply, and neither measure is an outage rate. An asset could participate in a system with changing generation shares while its own performance requires a separate operational record. That is why a reader should see the interval and geography beside every headline number.

### Commissioning, registration and later service need separate records

The Victorian Big Battery dossier separates Energy Safe Victoria's fire-investigation material from AEMO registration and operational information. Its documented questions concern commissioning and isolation, system-protection participation and emergency reserve controls. The inherited account leaves corrective-action closure and a compatible annual operating series unresolved. It does not turn the chronology into a general safety or reliability verdict.

This structure is useful because an incident investigation and market registration answer different questions. An investigation can address an event or condition; registration can establish participation in a defined system. Later operation does not itself prove every corrective action was closed, and a corrective action does not establish how reliably the asset served customers over subsequent seasons.

### Launch activity illustrates another denominator problem

FAA's FY2025 account records 195 licensed launches, nine licensed reentries and eight licensed operations resulting in mishaps. These are separate measures. The occurrence of a mishap does not, from these aggregate counts alone, establish a weather cause, infrastructure failure or failed payload deployment. A named event would need its own scope and official findings.

The Space Coast environmental and licensing records concern particular sites and authorizations. The retained September 15 check located the Shuttle Landing Facility's formal site-licence renewal; launches elsewhere in the region neither supplied that instrument nor establish its operator-, vehicle- or mission-specific permissions. The October 9 follow-up remains a check for later amendments or disposition. Site authority, actual use and safety outcomes have different scopes and cannot be combined into one reliability trend.

### Restoration is a sequence that must be observed

A useful recovery account distinguishes return of equipment, restoration of a service and restoration of service quality for affected users. The relevant event record would identify the outage boundary, affected customers or operations, temporary arrangements, the authority allowing return and the later observation period. These distinctions make the story useful to people evaluating dependable service rather than merely an asset's physical presence.

They also prevent hindsight from supplying an unsupported explanation. If output resumes after a repair, the sequence can be documented without asserting that the repair caused complete recovery. A stronger causal account would need supporting findings and alternative explanations. The September 1 sources are used here to frame that test, not to fill it with assumed outcomes.

### The next evidence should connect events to persistent service

For the battery cases, useful additions include final event findings, item-level corrective-action acceptance and repeated availability or dispatch records with stable asset identity. For space infrastructure, the corresponding records include the precise operating authorization, event disposition and later mission or service observations. Climate evidence should be joined only where the event and method support that connection.

The intended result is a profile that explains what conditions an asset encountered, how it performed and what remains uncertain about recovery. It would retain both favorable and adverse evidence without creating a universal resilience score. At present, system aggregates and named case records support bounded pieces of that profile, not a complete climate-attributed account of reliability.
