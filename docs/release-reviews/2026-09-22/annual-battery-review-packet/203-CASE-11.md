## Complete current reading

Gateway's inherited source shelf combines annual equipment data, an audit, an operator response and a regulatory resolution. It is well suited to explaining the difference between identified conditions, claimed corrective work and later operating evidence. The report does not turn those materials into a single judgment of safety or performance.

### Read findings and responses with attribution

The CPUC audit identifies conditions requiring response. The operator's response provides its account of corrective actions and risk, which remains attributed until the relevant regulator or later evidence verifies it. Annual EIA information can help identify the equipment but does not determine the operating implications of the audit. Readers should preserve the date and scope of each document so that a later response is not described as though it had already resolved every earlier finding. The distinction is essential to an accurate chronology.

### A resolution does not answer every performance question

CPUC Resolution E-5428 was adopted and effective on November 20, 2025; its cover transmittal is dated November 24. The operative order approved the amended Gateway contract and related costs. The summary table distinguishes 75 MW nameplate from 55.5 MW capacity value for mid-term reliability compliance. The amendment gives June 1, 2027 as expected initial delivery, with an August 1 deadline; the resolution says capacity and price remain as previously approved. These are contractual terms, not 75 MW of demonstrated restoration. Its historical account reports partial return while the fire-affected building remained offline and investigations were ongoing at that 2025 snapshot. The contracts cover portions of output, not specified facility resources. Later physical return, investigation closure, annual availability and customer reliability require separate evidence; this decision establishes none of their September 2026 status.

### The next reporting step

A stronger case would link individual findings to dated corrective evidence, verification and any relevant operating authorization. A subsequent period of service should identify available equipment, scheduled and actual activity, interruptions and measurement definitions. Safety and financial conclusions would remain separate. This would let readers distinguish a reported correction from verified completion and continuing operation. The September profile supplies the source trail and the questions to resolve; it does not infer a current stage, assign a failure classification or compare Gateway with other batteries on incompatible evidence.
