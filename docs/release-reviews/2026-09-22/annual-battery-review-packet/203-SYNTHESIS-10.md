## Complete current reading

An incident, an investigation, a company's corrective-action response and a regulator's closure decision are different records. A trustworthy safety account identifies which one supports each statement and avoids turning later activity into proof of recovery. The inherited Moss Landing and Victorian Big Battery cases provide concrete examples. Mineral and financing sources remain relevant background to industrial expansion, but they contain no incident finding and cannot establish that a particular project failed or restarted.

### Identify the affected asset before classifying an event

A shared facility name can conceal different units, processes and regulatory scopes. Before interpreting an incident, the reader needs the exact equipment or service involved, the event date, the reporting authority and the affected boundary. A conventional power-plant audit and a battery-fire investigation may concern the same broader site while documenting different conditions.

The distinction determines what a later correction can establish. Closing an item in one audit does not close every safety question at the site. A careful chronology therefore preserves the scope of each finding and response. It should not use a site's name as an identity join when the underlying records concern different systems.

### Moss Landing shows why the issuer matters

The inherited Moss Landing signal describes a CPUC-posted company response to a conventional plant audit. The response labels 13 of 28 findings complete and 15 in progress. These are the company's item-level response positions. They are not final regulator closure, and the document is not the final investigation of the January 2025 battery fire.

This is still useful evidence: it gives a denominator and a differentiated account of corrective work. The appropriate next step is to find the reviewing authority's disposition for the relevant items and identify any remaining obligations. Treating every company-complete label as regulatory acceptance would remove precisely the distinction the document makes possible to inspect.

### Victoria's case separates investigation from operation

The Victorian Big Battery dossier preserves Energy Safe Victoria material alongside AEMO registration and operational records. It identifies commissioning and isolation work, protection-scheme registration and emergency reserve controls as separate parts of the case. Its open questions include corrective-action closure and annual availability, dispatch, cycling and other later operating measures.

Those records should be read as a sequence of differently scoped evidence. Registration may establish participation in a market or protection arrangement; an investigation concerns an event and its findings. Neither automatically establishes the long-term performance of the other. The inherited account does not assign a universal safety grade or conclude that one event explains every subsequent operating condition.

### Industrial importance does not settle a safety question

USGS's 2026 summary describes national mineral production and import reliance. DOE's Thacker Pass financing record describes a closed loan and designed lithium-carbonate output. These documents help explain why mineral-processing projects attract attention. They do not document a Thacker Pass incident, a failed test, a corrective action or a restart, and this chapter makes none of those claims.

The Nevada permit case does identify pre-operation inspection, monitoring and reporting requirements. Such conditions can guide future evidence collection without implying a violation. A requirement and a finding of noncompliance are different statements. Public importance, investment scale or supply-chain urgency should not change the evidentiary test applied to either favorable or adverse project claims.

### A return to activity is not a complete recovery measure

A restart account should distinguish permission to resume, the actual resumption of activity and sustained performance afterward. It should identify temporary limits, affected equipment, the accepted remedy and the later observation window. A single operating event may demonstrate resumption without showing that normal capacity, service quality or risk controls persisted.

This is an analytical framework, not a finding that any named operator has unresolved unsafe conditions. Causal language should remain attached to the authority or analysis that supports it. If a repair precedes resumption, the chronology alone establishes sequence. Explaining the repair's effect requires evidence about the mechanism, alternatives and later condition of the same system.

### The next edition should make correction status verifiable

For Moss Landing, the strongest addition would be a reviewing authority's item-level disposition tied to the correct audit, alongside separate records for the battery-fire investigation. For Victorian Big Battery, it would be accepted corrective-action closure and a compatible subsequent operating series. Both require stable asset identities, exact dates and clear attribution of who considers an action complete.

A useful public casebook would then explain the initial condition, the official finding, the response, the acceptance basis and what later evidence shows. It should also state what remains unknown. The present baseline contains meaningful pieces of that story, but it does not authorize a new formal failure, recovery or safety classification merely because the chapter has been expanded.
