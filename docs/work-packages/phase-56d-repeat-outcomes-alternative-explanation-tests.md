## Test contract

Every entity test preserves:

- the stable Phase 56B panel and Phase 56C dossier;
- the named Phase 56C alternative explanations under test;
- two later or boundary observations;
- entity, unit, denominator, method, and observation-window compatibility;
- explicit source and claim attribution;
- independent-validation status;
- the unresolved result and next compatible records;
- an explicit statement that the record does not establish causation.

## Evidence boundaries

- Open recommendation counts and annual FISMA ratings remain separate records; neither is an agency performance score.
- Company roles, certifications, supplier clauses, and management systems do not substitute for same-line output, labor, yield, scrap, downtime, cost, delivery, or demand.
- FDA device-event counts lack a units-distributed and line denominator and are not defect rates.
- Regulator-verified obligations, removal progress, incidents, investigations, and partial return remain distinct from complete closure, availability, dispatch, cycling, degradation, revenue, grid service, and customer outcomes.
- DOT monthly operating-carrier tables and brand-level complaint counts use different denominators. Complaint records may include partner-operated flights.
- No test creates a causal effect, ranking, composite score, readiness score, or unsupported cross-entity comparison.

## Stop rules

- Stop interpretation when the entity, unit, denominator, method, observation window, or attribution level changes materially.
- Keep regulator verification separate from operator and company claims.
- Do not infer closure from an obligation, response, partial milestone, or missing later finding.
- Do not treat a recommendation count, certification, supplier requirement, device-event count, incident, monthly cause mix, complaint count, capacity figure, or partial return as a performance score.
- Do not rank entities or create composite, causal, or readiness scores.
