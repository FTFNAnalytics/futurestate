## What Changed

Toronto's adopted 2026-2035 Water capital plan supplies a current citywide infrastructure and finance record.

The plan totals $18.893 billion in gross capital programming. It allocates the largest shares to underground infrastructure and plants and facilities, adds funding for sewer rehabilitation and water-treatment facilities, and describes capital-delivery constraints.

The plan also forecasts reserve balances falling below 2% of capital investment over the ten-year period, which it says limits flexibility for pricing changes and unplanned infrastructure needs.

## Why It Matters

This record adds the municipal system behind project-level servicing decisions. Toronto's housing pipeline depends on water, wastewater, stormwater, treatment, pumping, storage, and asset-renewal programs being financed and delivered.

It also shows why an approved budget is only one stage of infrastructure capacity.

## Delivery Stack

- Council-adopted program and annual cash flow
- Detailed project design and procurement
- Contract award and construction
- Commissioning and operating staff
- System performance and asset condition
- Site-specific service review and connection

## Evidence Limits

The $18.893 billion figure is a ten-year gross program, not available cash, spare system capacity, or funding for application 24 254930.

The plan does not establish local hydraulic capacity, wastewater allocation, a project agreement, procurement completion, or an in-service asset. Its reserve forecast is a planning warning, not proof that a particular project will be delayed.

## What To Watch Next

Watch annual cash-flow revisions, material project reallocations, procurement awards, construction and commissioning, service measures, and project-specific servicing conditions.
