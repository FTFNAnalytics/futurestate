## What Changed

FTFN split a commodity-specific gallium record from the broader Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 baseline. USGS states that low-purity, unrefined gallium is not produced in the United States and that demand is supplied through imports, primarily from Canada, China, Germany, and Japan.

USGS also reports that about 79% of gallium consumed in the United States is in gallium arsenide, gallium nitride, and gallium phosphide wafers, and that analog and digital integrated circuits account for 74% of consumption.

## Why It Matters

This turns “critical minerals” into a specific dependency chain. Gallium supply and high-purity processing sit upstream of compound-semiconductor wafers used in integrated circuits, power and radio-frequency electronics, optoelectronics, and defense-related systems.

The 2026 commodity material also records the policy sensitivity of this chain: export controls and later temporary changes in restrictions can alter access conditions without changing the underlying concentration of processing capacity.

## Dependency Stack

- Gallium-bearing bauxite and zinc processing streams
- Low-purity gallium recovery
- High-purity refining
- Trade and export licensing
- GaAs, GaN, and GaP wafer production
- Device fabrication and qualification
- Recycling, substitution, inventory, and supplier diversification

## Evidence Limits

Import-supplied demand is not the same as a current shortage. The USGS evidence does not show a disruption at a named fab, supplier, or defense program, nor does it quantify company inventories, contracted supply, qualification timelines, or the feasibility of proposed domestic projects.

The consumption shares describe the national use structure. They do not prove that every gallium-dependent device has the same exposure or that substitution is impossible in every application.

## What To Watch Next

Watch updated import-source shares, export-license policy, primary and high-purity production outside China, U.S. recovery and refining projects, wafer-supplier disclosures, recycling and substitution evidence, and any named facility procurement or qualification record.
