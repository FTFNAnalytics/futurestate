## What Changed

NIST's Metrology Exchange to Innovate in Semiconductors, or METIS, provides a named data-exchange layer for CHIPS Metrology research.

NIST says the beta launched with initial data from three projects: high-speed metrology for magnetoelectronic devices and models, multiscale modeling and validation of semiconductor materials and devices, and CalNet.

## Why It Matters

Metrology is part of semiconductor manufacturing infrastructure. Measurements, models, calibration, and uncertainty determine whether researchers and manufacturers can compare results and control increasingly complex processes.

METIS is therefore more useful than a generic program announcement: it identifies an actual delivery surface where research data can be made available.

## Readiness Stack

- Research projects produce documented data
- Data receive stable identifiers, metadata, methods, and versioning
- Users can discover, retrieve, and interpret the records
- Additional CHIPS Metrology projects become represented
- External researchers test reuse and interoperability
- Manufacturers validate methods against production needs

## Evidence Limits

The beta launch is not proof that all CHIPS Metrology results are available, that the datasets interoperate, or that manufacturers use them.

It also does not establish higher yields, lower costs, improved reliability, or new fab capacity. Those outcomes require dataset-level use evidence and manufacturing validation.

## What To Watch Next

Watch dataset additions, access methods, version history, citations, standards links, external use, and evidence that a METIS result changes a measurement or manufacturing workflow.
