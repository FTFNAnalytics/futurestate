## What Changed

Natural Resources Canada's 2026-27 departmental plan gives the Canadian Critical Minerals Strategy a current spending and delivery frame.

The horizontal initiative lists $432.842 million in planned 2026-27 spending. It says the government intends to finalize agreements from the 2025-26 Critical Minerals Infrastructure Fund call, launch a First and Last Mile Fund, continue geoscience and data work, and pursue a target under which 60% of 19 measured minerals increase production by at least 10% from a 2022 baseline by December 2030.

## Why It Matters

This is a stronger monitoring record than a strategy headline because it names current-year work, spending, program instruments, indicators, and a dated target.

It also makes the conversion boundary explicit. A federal plan can organize data, agreements, and infrastructure support without yet creating a permitted mine, processing plant, transportation link, or unit of production.

## Delivery Stack

- Program allocation and call design
- Selected project and contribution agreement
- Permitting and consultation
- Power, road, rail, and community infrastructure
- Financing and construction
- Processing and commissioning
- Reported operating output

## Evidence Limits

Planned spending is not actual spending. An agreement under development is not a signed agreement. A signed agreement is not a permit, construction milestone, or operating result.

The national production target aggregates multiple minerals and does not establish that any individual mineral, region, or project will meet it.

## What To Watch Next

Watch signed project agreements, named Ontario projects, permit decisions, infrastructure awards, construction milestones, commissioning, actual program spending, and reported production against the 2030 indicator.
