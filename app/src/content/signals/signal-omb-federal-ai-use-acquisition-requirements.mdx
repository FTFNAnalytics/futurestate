## What Changed

FTFN selected OMB memoranda M-25-21 and M-25-22 as a paired federal AI implementation record.

M-25-21 addresses agency use of AI. M-25-22 addresses the acquisition of AI systems and services. Read together, they turn adoption into a stack of operational questions rather than a single policy instruction.

## Why It Matters

M-25-22 tells covered agencies to update acquisition procedures, involve cross-functional officials, address privacy and intellectual-property rights, avoid costly vendor dependencies, and obtain documentation that supports performance monitoring and explainability.

It also applies its acquisition requirements to solicitations issued 180 days after the memorandum and to later options that renew or extend existing contracts. That makes the memorandum a dated procurement rail, not only general guidance.

## Implementation Stack

- Define the covered AI use and identify high-impact uses
- Set measurable performance and risk requirements
- Bring acquisition, technology, cybersecurity, privacy, legal, civil-rights, data, and program officials into the process
- Address data ownership, training use, portability, interfaces, and vendor lock-in
- Preserve documentation and monitoring access for the life of the system
- Reassess performance, effectiveness, and risk after award

## Evidence Limits

The memoranda establish executive-branch requirements and recommendations. They do not prove that a particular agency has updated its procedures, that a procurement is compliant, that a system performs well, or that AI adoption has improved government services.

They also exclude or treat some systems and activities differently. Any later claim needs the covered agency, procurement, use case, contract, implementation evidence, and current policy status.

## What To Watch Next

Watch agency acquisition procedures, AI use-case inventories, contract clauses, performance assessments, waivers, audits, and later OMB amendments or rescissions.
