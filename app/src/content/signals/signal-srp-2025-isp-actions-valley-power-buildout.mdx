## What Changed

FTFN selected SRP's 2025 Integrated System Plan Actions Progress Report as the first named utility-planning record in the Southwest chip-corridor dossier.

The report covers progress on ten actions during fiscal year 2025. It connects SRP's system strategies to resource selection, distribution enablement, proactive siting, regional transmission, customer programs, pricing, and coal-transition work.

## Why It Matters

The corridor's power question is no longer represented only by a statewide electricity baseline or a generic utility-planning page. This record shows how one Valley utility says it is responding to system growth through procurement, construction, operating systems, land preparation, and transmission analysis.

The report states that SRP must at least double resource capacity by 2035. It also records construction underway for 575 MW of flexible natural-gas resources and 55 MW of solar, plus executed or developing agreements from all-source procurement processes. Its transmission section describes modeled 2035 upgrade needs rather than completed capacity.

## Dependency Stack

- Resource selections must become executed agreements
- Agreements must become permitted, financed, built, and commissioned assets
- New resources need transmission and deliverability
- Distribution systems must support changing loads and customer connections
- Customer projects still require service-territory, tariff, interconnection, and load evidence

## Evidence Limits

This is SRP's own implementation report and should be treated as interested-party primary evidence. System-wide megawatts, studies, and procurement activity do not prove spare capacity or a service commitment at a particular industrial site.

The report does not identify a semiconductor facility load, interconnection position, service agreement, tariff outcome, delivery date, or project-specific reliability condition.

## What To Watch Next

Watch later ISP action reports, executed resource agreements, construction and commissioning milestones, transmission filings, service-territory records, large-load tariffs, and customer-specific interconnection or service evidence.
