## What Changed

SRP's E-67 Standard Price Plan became effective with the November 2025 billing cycle for new large-load accounts served through a dedicated or customer-owned substation. A large-load account is one with a load of at least 20 MW or one SRP reasonably expects, based on customer forecasts, to reach 20 MW within five years.

The price plan generally sets minimum billing demand at 80 percent of forecast demand. It also says SRP can require customer reimbursement when a customer asks the utility to accelerate load-serving resources or infrastructure that SRP would not otherwise procure on that cost or schedule.

## Why It Matters

Phase 51A showed system-wide grid action. E-67 adds the commercial and operational boundary for a class of large customers: adequate facilities, forecasts, maximum-load limits, a service agreement, and potential customer-funded infrastructure all sit between regional power planning and delivered service.

## Dependency Stack

- A proposed account must meet the tariff's applicability rules
- Adequate facilities must be available
- The customer must provide demand forecasts
- SRP determines maximum load and service conditions
- A service agreement can define billing demand, maximum load, and forecasting rules
- Expedited resources or infrastructure may require customer reimbursement

## Evidence Limits

This is a tariff, not a customer service agreement. It does not name TSMC, a supplier, a data center, or another semiconductor project.

Eligibility for E-67 does not prove that sufficient generation, transmission, distribution, substation, or interconnection capacity exists at a specific site. The tariff itself begins with the condition that facilities of adequate capacity be available.

## What To Watch Next

Watch E-67 revisions, the associated Facilities Rider, named electric-service agreements, project load disclosures, utility construction records, and any project-specific interconnection or energization milestone.
