## What Changed

The IEA reported in April 2026 that global data-centre electricity consumption grew by 17% in 2025. Its analysis also points to physical bottlenecks, including long lead times for transformers and turbines, grid-connection queues, and approval timelines. EIA electricity data and Form 861 provide official U.S. utility context for the next layer of analysis.

## Why It Matters

AI scaling depends on generation, transmission, distribution, interconnection, cooling, water, land, permitting, and local legitimacy. The dated IEA update makes the infrastructure constraint more concrete without implying that every region, utility, or data-centre proposal faces the same conditions.

## Dependency Stack

- Power supply and grid reliability
- Interconnection and utility planning
- Data center construction and cooling
- Semiconductor supply
- Water and land-use approvals
- Public legitimacy in host communities

## Evidence Limits

The 17% figure is an aggregate data-centre measure reported by the IEA, not a local forecast and not a direct measure of AI-only consumption. Form 861 is annual and lagged. Neither source proves that a specific utility can absorb a new facility or that a proposed project has power, water, land-use, or permit approval.

Track specific electricity datasets, utility filings, data-center siting decisions, and local permitting signals that turn global AI demand into local infrastructure pressure.
