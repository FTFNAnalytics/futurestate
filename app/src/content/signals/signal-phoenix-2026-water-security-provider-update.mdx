## What Changed

FTFN selected Phoenix Water Services' April 28, 2026 council update as the first named provider-level water record in the Southwest chip-corridor dossier.

The City describes a portfolio drawing from the Salt and Verde rivers, the Colorado River, and groundwater. It also identifies stored water recovery, infrastructure to move water across the system, additional groundwater capacity, renewable surface-water supplies, and Pure Water Phoenix as parts of its reliability strategy.

## Why It Matters

Statewide water rules cannot answer every local capacity question. A municipal provider record helps identify which institution operates the system, what sources it relies on, how it describes current drought conditions, and which investments it says are intended to strengthen reliability.

The City reported that its drought plan remained at Stage 1, focused on awareness and conservation, while warning that Colorado River conditions and future federal operating guidelines remain consequential.

Phoenix's 2025 Water Quality Report adds the municipal system's current regulated drinking-water monitoring layer. It records detected-substance ranges and states that the system met applicable standards. That is useful public-system evidence, but it addresses water quality rather than industrial capacity.

## Dependency Stack

- River supplies and allocation rules
- Groundwater rights, wells, storage, and recovery
- Treatment, conveyance, and distribution infrastructure
- Reclaimed and advanced-purification water projects
- Conservation and shortage-response operations
- Site-specific demand, connection, rate, discharge, and reuse agreements

## Evidence Limits

The update is Phoenix's own account of provider planning and system resilience. It does not establish that a named fab, supplier, data center, or other industrial project can obtain a particular volume of water at a particular location or schedule.

The 2025 Water Quality Report does not change those limits. It does not disclose a project water budget, hydraulic model, meter or main capacity, service agreement, wastewater discharge condition, rate analysis, or facility reuse commitment.

## What To Watch Next

Watch the next Water Resource Plan, drought-stage changes, infrastructure budgets and council presentations, Pure Water Phoenix milestones, Colorado River operating rules, and site-specific industrial service or reuse records.
