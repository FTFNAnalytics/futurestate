## What Changed

Toronto's 2025 Development Pipeline adds a citywide stage model to the Ontario dossier. The City reports 791,045 proposed residential units in projects active from 2021 through 2025, with separate categories for projects under review, approved, pursuing building permits, holding issued permits, under construction, and built.

The bulletin reports 374,786 units under review, 294,017 approved, and 113,142 pursuing building permits. It also cites 231,457 net CMHC completions since 2011 and says that converting potential supply into completed homes depends on the pace of delivery and market conditions.

## Why It Matters

The dataset supplies the system-level denominator around application `24 254930`. A project can move through planning while the citywide evidence still shows a much larger gap between potential supply and completed homes.

## Dependency Stack

- Applications must be reviewed and approved
- Approved projects must seek and receive building permits
- Financing and market conditions must support a start
- Labor, materials, and infrastructure must sustain construction
- Projects must reach completion and occupancy

## Evidence Limits

The pipeline is not a completion forecast. Proposed and approved units can be revised, delayed, appealed, financed later, or never built.

The dataset covers larger projects requiring planning approvals and excludes some as-of-right development below the site-plan-control threshold. Citywide totals also do not establish the current permit or financing status of application `24 254930`.

## What To Watch Next

Track quarterly pipeline updates, stage conversion rates, Toronto starts and completions, project cancellations, financing conditions, and the first building-permit record for application `24 254930`.
