## What Changed

The Arizona Corporation Commission's February 4, 2026 open-meeting summary records a unanimous vote approving a Certificate of Environmental Compatibility for Baccara Power, LLC in docket `L-21369A-25-0222-00253`.

The proposed Project Baccara configuration includes two data-center buildings and approximately 700 MW of on-site natural-gas generation in western Maricopa County. The Commission says the generation would serve the data center and support the regional grid.

The same record relays the developer's plan to use chilled rather than evaporative cooling and to use wastewater from a nearby brewery in a reclaimed-water system. It also says Maricopa County and Luke Air Force Base approvals remained pending.

The trail advanced again at the county level. KJZZ reported that the Maricopa County Board of Supervisors voted 4-1 on May 6, 2026 to issue the military compatibility permit. The official Board agenda for case `MCP250007` sets conditions rather than a completed project state: an annexation/service agreement or provider will-serve letter, a later precise Plan of Development before construction permits, fire approvals, a Military Compatibility Compliance Report coordinated with Luke Air Force Base, FAA/FCC and DoD evidence, air-quality permits, final engineering, water-system approvals, and later building and occupancy steps.

An official Maricopa County Air Quality Department notice records proposed Permit `P0013417` for Takanock Baccara LLC, facility `F053690`. The notice records a March 4-April 8, 2026 public-comment period and says the Department was proposing to issue the permit. It does not record a final issuance.

## Why It Matters

This record now shows a more complete local conversion stack: a large compute load, dedicated generation, a state environmental-compatibility decision, a conditional county land-use decision, a proposed air permit, service and engineering prerequisites, cooling and reuse plans, and later construction and occupancy gates.

## Evidence Limits

The ACC explicitly distinguishes its CEC jurisdiction from approval of the data center itself. The CEC does not prove that the generation plant, interconnection, data-center buildings, or water infrastructure have been financed, permitted at every level, constructed, commissioned, or operated.

The cooling and wastewater statements are developer plans relayed in an official Commission summary. They are not measured water withdrawals, reuse volumes, discharge records, completed infrastructure, or proof of near-zero groundwater consumption.

The accessible County agenda is not the fully executed MCP, so the 4-1 outcome is independently corroborated rather than represented as a minute extracted from that document. The proposed air permit is not an issued permit or EPA non-objection. None of these records proves that the conditions have been satisfied.
