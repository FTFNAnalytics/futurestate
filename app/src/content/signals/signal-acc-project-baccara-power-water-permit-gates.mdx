## What Changed

The Arizona Corporation Commission's February 4, 2026 open-meeting summary records a unanimous vote approving a Certificate of Environmental Compatibility for Baccara Power, LLC in docket `L-21369A-25-0222-00253`.

The proposed Project Baccara configuration includes two data-center buildings and approximately 700 MW of on-site natural-gas generation in western Maricopa County. The Commission says the generation would serve the data center and support the regional grid.

The same record relays the developer's plan to use chilled rather than evaporative cooling and to use wastewater from a nearby brewery in a reclaimed-water system. It also says Maricopa County and Luke Air Force Base approvals remained pending.

The trail advanced again at the county level. Maricopa County's official May 6, 2026 meeting summary records an approve motion that passed by a 4-1 voice vote, moved by District 4 Supervisor Lesko and seconded by District 1 Supervisor Stewart. That converts the previously reported outcome into an official Board action record.

The official Board agenda for case `MCP250007` still defines a conditional conversion path rather than a completed project state: an annexation/service agreement or provider will-serve letter, a later precise Plan of Development before construction permits, fire approvals, a Military Compatibility Compliance Report coordinated with Luke Air Force Base, FAA/FCC and DoD evidence, final engineering, water-system approvals, and later building and occupancy steps.

The air-permit trail has also converted. MCAQD's AQ Data Viewer lists Title V Permit `P0013417` for Takanock Baccara LLC, facility `F053690`, as Active and Issued Final. The record gives June 30, 2026 as the effective and final issuance date, June 30, 2031 as the expiration date, and includes a signed final permit uploaded July 1, 2026.

## Why It Matters

This record now shows a more complete local conversion stack: a large compute load, dedicated generation, a state environmental-compatibility decision, an official county approval, a final active air permit, service and engineering prerequisites, cooling and reuse plans, and later construction and occupancy gates.

## Evidence Limits

The ACC explicitly distinguishes its CEC jurisdiction from approval of the data center itself. The CEC does not prove that the generation plant, interconnection, data-center buildings, or water infrastructure have been financed, permitted at every level, constructed, commissioned, or operated.

The cooling and wastewater statements are developer plans relayed in an official Commission summary. They are not measured water withdrawals, reuse volumes, discharge records, completed infrastructure, or proof of near-zero groundwater consumption.

The County summary confirms the motion and vote, but it is not the fully executed MCP and does not prove that any condition has been satisfied.

The signed final air permit closes the proposed-permit gate. It does not prove that the power plant or data center has begun or completed construction, passed required testing, secured every other approval, received occupancy authorization, or entered operation. No operating emissions or performance result is inferred from permit issuance.
