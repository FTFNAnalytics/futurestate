## What Changed

Executive Order 14412, dated June 22, 2026, directs federal PQC leadership and migration work. It requires agency migration leads, sets December 31, 2030 for PQC key establishment on high-value assets and high-impact systems, sets December 31, 2031 for digital signatures on those systems, directs a NIST pilot by the end of 2027, and calls for procurement and critical-infrastructure guidance.

OMB M-26-15, dated June 24, 2026, supplies an implementation layer for non-national-security federal systems. It requires agencies to develop PQC migration plans within 120 days, prioritize high-impact and high-value systems, integrate PQC into governance and modernization, address vendor and third-party software, and work toward mitigating as much quantum risk as feasible by December 31, 2030.

NIST's June 2026 PIV working set then exposes one technical implementation lane: proposed data, card-interface, and algorithm changes for adding ML-DSA and ML-KEM through a backward-compatible dual-stack approach. NIST labels the materials preliminary working drafts rather than formal drafts or final requirements.

## Why It Matters

The earlier NIST record showed that standards exist and migration work is necessary. These records identify who must lead, what plans must contain, which systems move first, where procurement enters the stack, when major transitions are expected, and how one federal credential standard may need to change.

## Evidence Limits

Requirements and preliminary drafts are not implementation results. The records do not show that agency inventories are complete, migration plans have been accepted, PIV changes are final, appropriations are available, products are validated, contracts have changed, or systems have moved successfully.

The OMB memorandum excludes national security systems. Critical-infrastructure assistance and future acquisition rules also require downstream guidance and rulemaking.
