## A mandate is assembled from separate decisions

Public authorization does not emerge automatically from a meeting, election, comment period, expert report, agency mandate, majority view, or completed procedure. Legitimacy depends on the authority exercised, people and rights affected, notice and access, evidence, alternatives, reasons, distribution, dissent, appeal, safeguards, duration, and capacity to revise the choice.

Phase 77 keeps each part inspectable. Standing identifies who must be able to enter the record. Notice and access determine whether that opportunity was usable. Deliberation organizes evidence and alternatives. Reasoned response shows how material issues affected the choice. Legitimacy audit examines authority and process. Appeal makes closure contestable. The mandate records the authorized terms. Adaptive review governs change.

## Participation is neither a vote nor decoration

Participation can reveal knowledge, values, rights, failure modes, burdens, alternatives, and conditions that would otherwise be invisible. It can also be unequal, captured, inaccessible, performative, or disconnected from the final decision.

Phase 77 measures reach, access, support, safety, evidence quality, alternatives, voice, influence, response, power imbalance, remedy, and process learning. These dimensions remain descriptive and evidence-linked. They do not produce a composite legitimacy score, rank communities, or convert comment counts into a mandate.

## State the mandate precisely

A future mandate record identifies the decision, authority, jurisdiction, scope, version, affected population, alternatives considered, public reasons, accepted evidence, unresolved uncertainty, conditions, safeguards, remedies, owner, resources, effective date, duration, review date, sunset, appeals, monitoring measures, triggers, and downstream obligations.

What is not authorized is as important as what is. A pilot mandate does not authorize scale. A permit does not prove operation. A consultation does not prove consent. A condition does not prove compliance. An initial benefit does not erase later harm.

## Make renewal an affirmative decision

Administrative systems often allow temporary decisions to become permanent through repeated extensions, budget continuation, contract renewal, or lack of a triggering complaint. Phase 77 disables silent renewal and automatic extension. Continued authorization after the review date requires an updated record and a separate decision.

The review considers operating evidence, benefits, harms, distribution, complaints, incidents, changed conditions, new publics, rights, implementation fidelity, alternatives, restoration, and exit capacity. The mandate can be continued with conditions, amended, paused, reversed, sunset, or retired.

## Connect review to remedy and retirement

Adaptive governance is incomplete if it can identify failure but cannot change course. A mandate therefore names escalation, stop, remedy, compensation, restoration, decommissioning, residual duties, and archive paths. These connect back to Phase 75 accountability and Phase 76 policy-retirement controls.

Retirement does not erase the original rationale, dissent, evidence, or harm. It closes defined authority only after transition and residual duties are reviewed and receipted.
