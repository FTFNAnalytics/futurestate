## The realization ladder

An institution can spend money, hire staff, install equipment, complete construction, accept an asset, deliver service, and still lack evidence of benefit. Each is meaningful, but none can silently stand in for the next.

Phase 75 preserves this ladder: commitment, activity, delivered output, acceptance, exposure, outcome, benefit or harm, contribution, and bounded attribution.

## Benefits and harms use the same discipline

Benefits, harms, burdens, opportunity costs, displacement, failure demand, and unintended effects share the same requirements for identity, unit, denominator, population, period, source, method, uncertainty, revision, and review.

Favorable evidence does not receive a lower admission threshold. Missing harm data does not become evidence of no harm.

## Distribution is part of the result

An average can conceal who receives service, who waits, who pays, who is displaced, who faces risk, and who lacks access. Distributional monitoring therefore records affected groups, geography, eligibility, exposure, benefit, harm, burden, missingness, and uncertainty.

Small groups are not removed merely because reporting is difficult. Privacy limits are recorded as constraints and may narrow the public claim.

## Counterfactual decision audit

The audit asks not only whether an outcome changed, but what would plausibly have happened under the status quo or another available option. It reviews timing, co-interventions, external shocks, implementation variation, selection, spillovers, displacement, and alternative explanations.

When causal identification is unavailable, the record can support implementation, descriptive, association, or contribution language. It cannot leap to attribution.

## Net benefit is an adjudication

A net-benefit claim requires compatible benefit, harm, burden, distribution, uncertainty, and time-horizon records. It must disclose who made the tradeoff, which values were applied, and which outcomes were not monetized or compared.

Phase 75 disables automatic benefit attribution and automatic net-benefit claims. A favorable outcome cannot erase an adverse event, dissent, subgroup disparity, or unresolved challenge.
