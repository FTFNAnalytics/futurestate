## The corpus change

Phase 55T does not make thin topics useful by adding a fourth headline to each page. It makes them useful by giving readers multiple evidence stages and a clear next-record trail.

Climate now has global observations, seasonal forecasting, national transportation-risk synthesis, and an emissions-method boundary. Discovery Technologies now spans funded data systems, operating NISAR data, national elevation design, and ocean mapping and field operations. Each record says where observation stops and local interpretation begins.

Mobility and Aviation now have federal policy, a proposed oversight program, crash reporting, a specific recall, a final powered-lift operating rule, pilot selections, company certification claims, and scheduled tests. These are not collapsed into one readiness judgment.

Space now connects hardware assembly to an architecture definition, communications specifications, and a lunar power dependency. Manufacturing, agriculture, cybersecurity, and quantum add a national statistical baseline, a bounded breeding-award portfolio, a binding asset-visibility obligation, and a backup-algorithm standardization milestone.

## What is publishable

Ten records pass as independently useful source updates: they describe final assessments, public data availability, a specific recall, a current architecture, an official statistical baseline, bounded awards, a final rule, named pilot selections, a binding directive, and an algorithm selection with explicit limits.

Eight remain In Review because they are global baselines awaiting regional use, methods without a concrete comparison, program designs, proposals, annual summaries, technical specifications, or strategies upstream of execution.

## Why the boundaries matter

A global climate ranking is not a local loss. Public radar data is not institutional adoption. An architecture is not a manifest. A specification is not service. A final operating rule is not a certified aircraft. A pilot selection is not a flight. A research award is not a successful cultivar. A binding directive is not compliance. An algorithm selection is not a final standard.

Those distinctions are the content product: they let readers see both the evidence already available and the records needed to move a claim forward.

## Next conversion

The next useful work is downstream evidence. FTFN should find named decisions that use the observation rails, final regulatory and approval records for autonomy, executed lunar program and interface records, facility-level manufacturing evidence, breeding outputs and field trials, agency compliance evidence, and the HQC draft-standard trail.
