## The local conversion problem

National awards and company investment plans become local outcomes through a chain of municipal, utility, construction, workforce, and operating decisions. The chain must be assembled from records that apply to the same place and project. A prominent facility milestone can strengthen the dossier without closing the wider local-system question.

## Phoenix: a real milestone with open dependencies

Phoenix's Fab 3 topping-out record establishes that the building's superstructure reached a defined threshold. Existing city and company records also support production at Fab 1, construction progress at Fab 2, a large industrial wastewater agreement, workforce programs, and a broader multi-facility plan.

The combined evidence is stronger than an investment announcement. It still does not establish sufficient power, completed reclaimed-water infrastructure, certificates of occupancy, tool acceptance, workforce placement, qualified production, supplier localization, or local-system sufficiency for the full plan.

## Toronto: preserve the pre-decision boundary

Toronto's SC33/9 records identify a proposed development and an active municipal decision process. Until the scheduled council steps occur and official dispositions are posted, the project remains a proposal at a pre-decision gate. Even approval would remain upstream of permits, servicing, construction, occupancy, and operating use.

## A reusable local conversion sequence

For each project, preserve this sequence:

`proposal or funding` → `authority decision` → `permit and service agreements` → `construction` → `inspection and occupancy` → `equipment and commissioning` → `qualified operation` → `measured local outcomes`

Records can enter at different points, and a single record rarely proves the whole chain.
