## The project boundary can hide the system effect

Each project may satisfy its own permit, rate, servicing, safety, or operating requirement while several projects together exceed local grid, water, transport, housing, emergency-response, administrative, or workforce capacity. Phase 76 therefore adds a portfolio view without dissolving the identity of each decision.

Co-location is a reason to inspect interaction, not evidence that interaction exists.

## Trace incidence, not just totals

Portfolio benefit is reported by place, population, eligibility, exposure, timing, duration, payer, beneficiary, and uncertainty. The same applies to costs, congestion, displacement, outages, environmental burden, public expenditure, service delay, safety risk, and opportunity cost.

An aggregate gain cannot erase a neighborhood, user group, rate class, worker cohort, or future period carrying disproportionate burden. Missing subgroup evidence remains missing; it does not become an equal-distribution finding.

## Capacity has a time dimension

Temporary construction pressure, long-lived infrastructure demand, phased service, seasonal stress, maintenance outages, and emergency conditions can produce different cumulative effects. Reviewers compare when capacity becomes available against when demand and burden arrive.

A future mitigation project cannot be counted as current carrying capacity before it is delivered, accepted, and operating.

## Portfolio decisions need remedies

Governance options include resequencing, narrowing, conditioning, adding capacity, changing funding responsibility, protecting access, compensating affected groups, pausing new commitments, substituting a project, or exiting. The responsible authority must state who pays, who acts, and how compliance will be verified.

The [Local-System Burden portfolio](/evidence/learning/76-pgr-006-local-system-burden/) remains inactive. It contains no cumulative-burden finding, shared-capacity conclusion, distributional decision, rebalancing action, or remedy receipt.
