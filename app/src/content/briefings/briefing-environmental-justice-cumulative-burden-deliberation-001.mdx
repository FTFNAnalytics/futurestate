## The permit boundary is not the burden boundary

Public decisions are often organized by project, agency, environmental medium, asset, or fiscal program. People experience the combined system. A community may simultaneously face water demand, power infrastructure, traffic, noise, air emissions, land conversion, housing pressure, service demand, workforce displacement, climate hazard, or loss of access. Each individual authorization can appear bounded while the cumulative burden becomes material.

Phase 77 links deliberation to the Phase 76 portfolio register so shared dependencies and combined effects can be raised without assuming they have been proven. The issue record identifies the proposed pathway, geography, population, period, baseline, evidence, uncertainty, responsible authority, and missing information.

## Distribution matters independently of totals

A project or policy may produce a net public benefit while placing concentrated costs on people with less political, financial, physical, or institutional capacity to avoid them. An aggregate calculation does not answer who benefits, who pays, who is exposed, who is excluded, or whose existing burden is intensified.

Phase 77 keeps benefit, harm, burden, access, remedy, and distribution as separate findings. Decision makers must explain why a burden is accepted, reduced, shifted, compensated, restored, or left unresolved. They cannot treat jobs, revenue, capacity, or service gains as an automatic offset against unrelated health, rights, affordability, environmental, or cultural effects.

## Community evidence is evidence with method

Lived experience, complaints, local monitoring, oral histories, community surveys, land-based knowledge, administrative records, modeled exposure, inspections, and technical measurements can all contribute. Each has scope and limitations. Phase 77 records method, time, geography, denominator, missingness, provenance, privacy, correction, and disagreement rather than placing community and technical evidence in opposing categories.

Where evidence is incomplete, uncertainty remains visible. Missing measurement does not prove no burden, and concern alone does not establish magnitude or causation. The response must state what is known, what is disputed, what remains unmeasured, and what precaution, monitoring, or study is warranted.

## Hear from people before choosing the mitigation

Mitigation designed without affected-community participation may be unusable, mistimed, inaccessible, or directed at the wrong harm. The process should expose alternatives, thresholds, safeguards, benefit-sharing proposals, monitoring access, complaint routes, emergency procedures, compensation, restoration, and exit options early enough to change the decision.

A commitment becomes meaningful only when it has an owner, authority, resources, measure, baseline, deadline, verification method, consequence, and review path. Phase 77 links future conditions back to implementation and impact evidence rather than treating them as closure.

## Reopen when burdens change

Adaptive triggers include new exposure data, a service failure, a material incident, threshold exceedance, changed population, unequal benefit delivery, unresolved complaint, failed safeguard, authority change, or cumulative interaction with another project. Reopening is a separate decision with renewed notice and standing review.

The mandate may be continued, conditioned, paused, amended, reversed, sunset, or retired. None of those outcomes is automatic.
