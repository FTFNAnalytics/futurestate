## The operating domain is part of the evidence

Autonomous performance cannot be detached from where, when, how, and for whom the system operated. Roads, weather, lighting, traffic mix, communications, supervision, facility rules, mission profile, software version, fallback strategy, and human response all shape the observed result.

A safe record in one operating domain is not a permission slip for another.

## Exposure comes before rates

Counts of incidents, interventions, complaints, or successful trips are uninterpretable without exposure. The comparison needs compatible denominators, service periods, eligibility rules, missingness, exclusions, severity definitions, reporting obligations, and version histories.

Phase 76 rejects a transfer when one side reports vehicle miles and another reports trips, operating hours, missions, or selected events unless an independently reviewed compatibility method exists.

## Regulation and operation stay separate

A regulator can authorize a service without proving its long-run safety, accessibility, reliability, or public value. An operator can complete trips without proving equal access or acceptable burden. A technical evaluation can identify capability without creating permission to deploy.

Cross-case learning retains the distinct actors: evaluator, developer, operator, authorizer, incident investigator, affected user, and remedy authority.

## Failure modes must remain visible

Fallback activation, degraded operation, unusual conditions, blocked access, complaint patterns, recalls, pauses, and corrective actions belong in the learning record. An aggregate success rate cannot erase a low-frequency high-severity event or a subgroup that cannot use the service.

The strongest reusable lesson may be a safeguard, stop condition, reporting rule, or reason not to expand the operating domain.
