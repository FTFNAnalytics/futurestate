## Why These Three Corridors

The systems were selected for evidence density, not narrative symmetry. Each has a dense official record, clear institutional authority, repeated relevance across FTFN topics, and named projects that can be followed over time.

Northern Virginia makes compute growth local through electricity, rate design, transmission, water, land, construction, and public acceptance. Nevada makes critical-minerals policy local through federal land decisions, state permits, water rights, federal credit, processing facilities, and customer qualification. The Space Coast makes launch growth local through site and operator licensing, environmental review, capital programs, range capacity, shared infrastructure, and mission operations.

## The Common Conversion Gate

Upstream evidence is strong in all three corridors:

- demand, forecasts, and policy in Northern Virginia;
- authorization, permits, and finance in Nevada;
- plans, site licenses, environmental decisions, and capital programs in Florida.

The common weak point is downstream. FTFN still needs named construction, inspection, acceptance, service, commissioning, compliant operation, utilization, and measured local outcomes.

## What The Comparison Does Not Mean

The corridors are not equivalent markets or technologies. The comparison is about evidence stages. A rate decision, mining ROD, and spaceport license are different legal instruments, but each can be misread as a completed outcome. This briefing keeps those boundaries explicit.
