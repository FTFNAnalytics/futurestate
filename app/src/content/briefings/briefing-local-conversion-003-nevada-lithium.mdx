## The conversion question

Nevada's lithium story should be told as two named project trails, not as a national reserve or capacity headline. The key question is whether authorized and financed facilities move through construction, environmental compliance, commissioning, customer qualification, and repeatable accepted output.

Thacker Pass has a federal land decision, state permits, a closed federal loan, a reclamation assurance record, and an air-permit monitoring contract. Rhyolite Ridge has a federal decision and an explicit water-permit pre-operation stack, while the captured air-permit file and loan trail retain narrower holds.

## What remains open

Authorization does not establish condition satisfaction. A permit does not establish construction or compliance. Financial close does not establish disbursement, completed facilities, or production. Projected nameplate capacity does not establish qualified material, customer acceptance, recovery, operating cost, or sustained sales.

The next decisive records are physical and operational: inspections, construction completion, water and air monitoring, commissioning tests, product specifications, qualified lots, customer receipts, repeated output, recovery, cost, sales, and environmental performance.

## Reader use

Read the corridor as **authorization → finance → build → commission → qualify → sell → sustain**. National mineral-security context explains why the projects matter; it cannot substitute for evidence that either project has delivered accepted material.
