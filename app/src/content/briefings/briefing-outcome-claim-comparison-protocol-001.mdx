## Why an outcome claim is a separate decision

Series admission says that a bounded set of observations may be treated as compatible. It does not decide that a result improved, worsened, met a target, caused an effect, or can be attributed to one actor or intervention.

Eight Phase 71 claim dockets remain **Not Ready - No Admitted Series**. Proposed claim text, claim type, supporting panels, adverse observations, alternatives, uncertainty, attribution, reviewers, receipts, and propagation fields are empty.

## Ten outcome-inference gates

| Gate | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Admitted series | Every supporting panel resolves to an admitted compatible series |
| Identity and scope | One exact entity, operating scope, authority, and lifecycle |
| Repeated periods | Coverage, cadence, missingness, partial periods, and sufficiency |
| Direction and magnitude | A declared calculation from admitted points only |
| Compatibility | Units, denominator, exposure, method, version, and revisions |
| Adverse evidence | Breaks, failures, exclusions, corrections, and missing periods |
| Alternatives | Plausible explanations and counterfactual limits |
| Attribution | Responsible actor, intervention, receiver, and no-transfer boundary |
| Uncertainty | Coverage, sensitivity, and interpretation limitations |
| Decision | Independent dual review, receipt, and complete propagation |

## Descriptive, attributable, and causal language

A descriptive statement may report a bounded, reviewed change under one declared contract. An attribution statement requires a defensible relationship between that change and a named actor or intervention. A causal claim requires a suitable counterfactual design and is never authorized by series continuity alone.

Phase 71 sets `causal_claim_allowed` to false in every empty docket. It does not imply that causal evidence can never exist; it prevents causal language before an explicit future method and decision exist.

## Ten comparison-eligibility gates

Cross-entity comparison requires a common question, measure, unit, denominator, exposure, period, cadence, method, lifecycle stage, break treatment, coverage, uncertainty, human decision, and propagation record. A shared topic or file template is not a common comparison contract.

Every comparison register remains **Embargoed - No Common Admitted Series**. Candidate peers, approved measures, approved comparisons, decisions, and receipts are empty.

## Scoring and ranking prohibition

A bounded comparison cannot silently become a composite score, readiness index, league table, or rank. Weight selection, missing-data treatment, normalization, directionality, uncertainty, and normative judgments would each require a separate public method and review. Phase 71 creates none of them.

## Publication boundary

The [Outcome Registry](/evidence/outcomes/) publishes the embargo and the missing decisions. It does not publish a value, trend, favorable or adverse outcome, peer comparison, score, ranking, attribution, forecast, or causal conclusion.
