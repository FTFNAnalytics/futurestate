Stable tenure requires enforceable rights, predictable costs, maintenance, due process, continuity, and protection from arbitrary loss.

## Evidence architecture

The Phase 85 chain begins only after verified community-resource security. It then separates housing need and physical delivery; tenure, provider model, ownership, and household affordability; homelessness, shelter, supportive housing, displacement, and the right to remain; then retrofit, climate and disaster housing, relocation, reconstruction, right to return, and long-horizon place stability. Every transition needs lawful authority, affected-public standing, complete denominators, distribution review, independent challenge, human decision, and a dated receipt.

## Need, supply, approvals, delivery, and occupancy

Count households and dwellings, not just proposals. Preserve geography, tenure, size, accessibility, price, condition, vacancy, demolition, conversion, completion, and occupancy. Planning permission can remove a legal barrier but cannot establish land control, servicing, finance, workforce, materials, construction, inspection, completion, occupancy, or a durable net addition. Approved units are not delivered homes.

## Habitability, accessibility, and neighborhood infrastructure

A home requires safe structure, water, sanitation, electricity, heating and cooling, clean indoor air, fire safety, privacy, sufficient space, accessibility, security, timely repair, and protection from toxins and climate hazards. Location matters: transport, food, school, care, work, parks, culture, public space, and digital service are part of usable housing. A completed unit can still fail as a home.

## Tenure, ownership, and household affordability

Separate rental, ownership, leasehold, public, social, nonprofit, cooperative, community-land-trust, Indigenous-governed, supportive, institutional, informal, and emergency tenure. Test enforceable rights, governance, maintenance, operating subsidy, reserves, transfer, failure, and continuity. An affordability label or average price is not household affordability; include income volatility, utilities, fees, taxes, insurance, debt, transport, care, food, deposits, moving, and the duration and expiry of protection.

## Homelessness, shelter, supportive housing, and displacement

Measure unsheltered, sheltered, hidden, doubled-up, and repeated homelessness without treating people as scores. A shelter bed is not stable housing. Trace accessibility, dignity, privacy, belongings, admission and exclusion, length of stay, permanent exits, returns, health, mortality, service continuity, and rights. Protect against eviction, foreclosure, encampment clearance, confiscation, criminalization, redevelopment displacement, and arbitrary relocation.

## Retrofit, climate, disaster, relocation, and reconstruction

Retrofit spending is not a safe and habitable dwelling; verify completed work and measured safety, health, accessibility, energy, water, cost, emissions, durability, and tenant protection. Disaster response must preserve tenure, affordability, land rights, claims, services, choice, compensation, and community consent. Temporary relocation is not a right to return, and reconstruction is not community recovery. Measure return, occupancy, social and cultural continuity, neighborhood systems, land capture, repeat displacement, and long-term wellbeing.

## Decision boundary

This registry is deliberately empty. It contains no verified Phase 84 predecessor, adopted housing-need baseline, land-use approval, delivered-home finding, occupancy finding, habitability or accessibility conclusion, tenure or household-affordability finding, allocation, shelter placement, supportive-housing outcome, displacement decision, retrofit result, relocation order, right-to-return finding, reconstruction or community-recovery conclusion, review, receipt, score, rank, Phase 64 advance, or operating-outcome change.
