## Standing is a decision about exposure and rights

Public processes often begin with a generic invitation to “stakeholders.” That label hides the central governance question: who is affected, how, with what rights, and with what ability to absorb or contest the consequences? A land-use change, grid connection, automated service, industrial facility, digital-control program, or policy retirement can create different publics at the same time. Residents may face local burdens; workers may face occupational or displacement risks; users may depend on service continuity; rights holders may possess legal or treaty standing; ratepayers may carry cost; and future users may inherit irreversible choices.

Phase 77 makes that classification explicit. Each constituency class needs a named basis in exposure, dependence, authority, rights, knowledge, burden, or intergenerational interest. Attendance, organizational size, campaign activity, professional status, or proximity to the convenor cannot substitute for that basis.

## Identify the missing public

The most visible participants are rarely the complete affected public. Shift workers may not attend evening hearings. Disabled users may be excluded by inaccessible formats or venues. Rural communities may lack broadband. Tenants, temporary workers, caregivers, small suppliers, non-English speakers, youth, and people concerned about retaliation may be absent even when the decision directly affects them.

The standing register therefore records who may be missing, why the process may not reach them, and what support is needed. Outreach is not complete when a message is sent. The relevant question is whether the people entitled to participate could discover, understand, access, and safely use the process in time to matter.

## Keep standing open to challenge

Standing decisions can be wrong. A convenor may overlook an exposure pathway, define geography too narrowly, treat indirect effects as immaterial, or confuse technical expertise with lived experience. Phase 77 preserves challenges, evidence, corrections, reviewer conflicts, and the version history of the constituency inventory.

A challenge does not automatically grant standing, and a denial does not erase the claim. A separate reviewer records the reason, evidence, unresolved uncertainty, and appeal path. This keeps the boundary contestable without making it unlimited or arbitrary.

## Distinguish representation from presence

One participant cannot silently stand in for everyone who shares a label. Representation requires a stated constituency, a basis for authority or accountability, known limits, and a way for represented people to disagree. Institutional representatives, elected officials, unions, advocacy groups, associations, technical experts, and self-identified individuals can all contribute, but their roles are not interchangeable.

Phase 77 preserves direct testimony, representative submissions, expert analysis, public-authority positions, and dissent as different record types. It does not convert the number of organizations or signatures into evidence of legitimacy.

## Standing does not determine the outcome

Recognizing a person or group as affected does not decide the merits, establish consent, allocate evidentiary weight, or grant a veto unless an applicable authority creates one. Standing secures a fair route into the record. Evidence quality, rights, legal duties, distributional effects, public reasons, and decision authority are evaluated separately.

That separation protects both inclusion and rigor. It prevents participation from becoming symbolic while preventing a crowded process from becoming an unstructured plebiscite.

## What remains empty

No Phase 76 cross-case finding has been admitted, and no real Phase 77 public decision question has opened. The eight standing registers therefore contain no constituency determination, rights finding, notice plan, accessibility plan, challenge decision, reviewer identity, or receipt. The framework is a destination for future evidence, not evidence that participation occurred.
