## Workforce is an operating system

Industrial and infrastructure plans frequently treat labor as a quantity: jobs announced, positions forecast, training seats funded, or contractors procured. Delivery depends on a more complicated system of occupations, experience, shift coverage, certification, supervision, safety culture, bargaining relationships, retention, housing, transportation, childcare, and institutional memory.

Phase 77 gives workers and their representatives standing to describe that system before a mandate is issued. It also distinguishes direct employees, union members, non-union workers, contractors, temporary staff, trainees, displaced workers, occupational-health professionals, and workers in connected suppliers. Their exposures and authority are not identical.

## Protect the conditions for candor

A process cannot claim meaningful worker participation when speaking creates a credible risk of retaliation, lost work, immigration consequences, contract penalties, professional discipline, or disclosure of personal health information. The notice and access plan therefore includes confidential channels, attribution choices, data retention limits, non-retaliation protections, scheduling across shifts, paid participation where authorized, and independent escalation.

Anonymous or aggregated evidence still needs provenance and method. Protection does not mean that unsupported claims become facts; it means the process can evaluate relevant operational evidence without forcing unnecessary personal exposure.

## Trace the supply chain without granting a veto

Suppliers can reveal qualification lead times, material constraints, tooling dependencies, small-business exposure, financing pressure, cybersecurity obligations, logistics risk, and single-source failure. Large vendors, local suppliers, public purchasers, and subcontractors may also possess conflicting incentives.

Phase 77 records their evidence, declared conflicts, contractual constraints, and proposed alternatives. Participation does not give a supplier authority over public goals, and procurement volume does not establish public benefit. The decision maker must explain how supply-chain evidence affected sequencing, safeguards, diversification, local capacity, or exit options.

## Put distribution into the issue matrix

An aggregate employment benefit can coexist with occupational hazards, unstable scheduling, wage inequality, contractor risk, displacement, inadequate training, or benefits captured outside the affected community. A facility can create high-skilled jobs while intensifying housing, transport, water, or service pressure.

The issue matrix separates job quantity, job quality, accessibility, training completion, retention, safety, commuting, supplier participation, local fiscal effects, and distribution. Comments on these issues remain linked to evidence and a reasoned response rather than collapsed into a single “workforce support” label.

## Keep commitments reviewable

Participation may produce proposed conditions: training obligations, safety controls, apprenticeship access, supplier standards, workforce-transition support, reporting, complaint mechanisms, or milestone reviews. A proposal is not a commitment. A commitment is not delivery. Delivery is not benefit.

Phase 77 links any future condition to the Phase 75 implementation and impact records and to an adaptive-review trigger. If a condition is missed or creates a new burden, the public mandate can be reopened through a separate decision.

## What remains empty

No worker or supplier standing decision, protected channel, hearing, issue, response, condition, mandate, or adaptive review exists in Phase 77. The record does not infer workforce support from an announcement, labor agreement, procurement, training program, or absence of public objection.
