## Capacity has several states

Industrial announcements routinely compress site selection, financing, construction, equipment installation, commissioning, qualification, accepted output, customer shipment, and recurring operation into one capacity number. Phase 76 does not compare those numbers until their states, periods, units, and acceptance authorities align.

The relevant lesson may concern construction delivery, utility readiness, qualification time, workforce formation, supplier risk, or accepted output. Each needs its own denominator and decision lineage.

## Diversification can conceal concentration

Adding facilities can reduce geographic dependence while increasing reliance on the same equipment vendors, specialty inputs, grid nodes, water systems, contractors, technical workforce, financing programs, or regulatory pathways. Portfolio governance therefore maps shared dependencies before claiming resilience.

The concentration question is not simply how many projects exist. It is whether a common shock can interrupt several of them at once and whether substitutes are actually qualified and accessible.

## Capacity must be usable

Installed assets do not prove usable supply. Reviewers need acceptance criteria, product mix, yield or quality boundary, customer qualification, maintenance, input availability, energy and water intensity, and operating recurrence. A production record that excludes rework, downtime, or rejected output cannot silently become an operating-rate claim.

Workforce counts receive the same scrutiny: training seats, hires, certified workers, retained workers, staffed shifts, and productive operating capacity are different measures.

## Portfolio safeguards

The governance record tests supplier substitution, inventory buffers, alternate logistics, utility contingencies, phased commitments, stop-work thresholds, repair capacity, and orderly exit. It also asks who bears the cost when redundancy is purchased and which communities absorb additional infrastructure or environmental burden.
