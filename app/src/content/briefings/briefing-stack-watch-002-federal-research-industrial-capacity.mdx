## The research angle

The collection changes the shape of the FTFN research queue. Frontier capability is still important, but the most consistent federal research angle is now the stack beneath it: tools, materials, production, energy, compute, communications, security, logistics, workforce, and institutional authority.

DARPA's FY2027 budget and office-wide solicitations show this at the research layer. The government-wide documents show it at the strategy and policy layer. The transmission study shows it at the infrastructure layer.

## What the documents support

The documents support a bounded conclusion: U.S. research and national-security policy are increasingly organized around the ability to build, secure, power, connect, scale, and sustain technology. They also support named follow-up questions for budgets, awards, acquisition, standards, grids, suppliers, permits, projects, and local systems.

## What they do not prove

They do not prove that requested budgets were enacted, that solicitations produced successful programs, that supply chains are secure, that domestic capacity is sufficient, that transmission will be built, or that advanced AI, nuclear, communications, and defense systems are operating as intended.
