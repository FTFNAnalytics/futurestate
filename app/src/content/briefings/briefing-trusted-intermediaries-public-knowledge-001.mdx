A delivery method for libraries, navigators, peers, community media, language access, disability access, two-way questions, correction, and appeal.

## The operating question

Community capacity is the ability of people and institutions in a place to sustain connection, knowledge, care, collective action, democratic voice, and recovery. It is not the number of buildings, organizations, memberships, volunteers, posts, plans, or events. Phase 83 requires named institutions, affected publics, lawful roles, accessible services, real capacity, durable resources, safeguards, review, and receipts.

## Institution inventory and public purpose

Record the institution, owner, governing body, mandate, service role, geography, population, facility, hours, access channels, workforce, volunteers, funding, assets, dependencies, referral relationships, and fallback. Separate public, nonprofit, cooperative, Indigenous, faith, cultural, informal, and commercial roles. A familiar name or physical address does not prove accessibility, trust, legitimacy, capacity, or continuity.

## Access, trust, welcome, and belonging

Test complete access: travel, hours, cost, disability, language, literacy, safety, privacy, cultural fit, capacity at arrival, return, follow-up, and fallback. Trust must be bounded to a population, purpose, time, evidence source, and dissent record. Attendance does not prove welcome; repeat use does not prove consent; proximity does not prove belonging; absence does not prove disengagement.

## Libraries, schools, clinics, parks, and public space

Anchor institutions can combine learning, care, cooling, connectivity, navigation, meeting space, public records, culture, food, recreation, and emergency refuge. Record each function separately, including staffing and hours. Parks and civic space require universal design, safe access, maintenance, public use, conflict protocols, programming, shade and weather protection, sanitation, and the right to gather without forced consumption.

## Culture, faith, Indigenous authority, and place

Cultural, faith, ceremonial, Indigenous, treaty, customary, and place-based institutions may hold care, memory, language, land, authority, trust, and emergency roles that a service inventory misses. Do not treat them as interchangeable vendors or extract knowledge without consent. Record jurisdiction, governance, data and knowledge sovereignty, protocol, community benefit, ownership, representation, contestation, and withdrawal rights.

## Cooperatives, commons, and community ownership

Community ownership requires a defined asset, members or beneficiaries, governance rights, capital structure, transfer restrictions, benefit allocation, risk, maintenance duty, conflict process, succession, and public accountability. Participation is not control. A cooperative label does not prove equitable voice, affordability, public purpose, or protection against capture and demutualization.

## Civic networks, mutual aid, and volunteer capacity

Map needs, offers, skills, assets, coordinators, facilities, storage, transport, communications, cash, supplies, procurement, training, safeguarding, privacy, and escalation. Volunteer count is not usable capacity. Test role clarity, supervision, workload, expenses, insurance, liability, accessibility, duty of care, worker substitution, burnout, respite, rotation, succession, and dignified exit. Informal support must not erase public obligations.

## Local media and the public-information ecosystem

Record local reporting, public records, meeting coverage, emergency information, service navigation, public data, community knowledge, language access, archives, two-way questions, and correction. Identify publisher, owner, funder, workforce, source provenance, conflicts, distribution, platform dependencies, advertising, safety risks, and correction procedures. Information volume, official notice, platform reach, or virality does not establish public knowledge or local trust.

## Trusted intermediaries and information access

Libraries, clinics, schools, navigators, local media, peer networks, cultural organizations, faith groups, and civic associations may translate complex public information. Document the purpose and limits of trust, training, conflicts, privacy, consent, language, disability access, non-digital service, question pathways, escalation, appeal, and remedy. Trusted delivery cannot replace authoritative records or independent verification.

## Isolation, exclusion, and civic participation

Measure unwanted isolation separately from chosen solitude, network size, attendance, or household composition. Examine repeated encounter, reciprocity, care, autonomy, accessible participation, safety, discrimination, displacement, digital exclusion, time, transport, money, institutional welcome, and the ability to influence decisions. Do not score belonging or rank communities.

## Preparedness, community hubs, and continuity

Preparedness requires a current risk and asset map, named owners, accessible warnings, community hubs, refuge, food, water, energy, communications, health, care, mobility, records, cash, supplies, repair, trained people, mutual aid, drills, fallback, and correction. A written plan is not readiness. Separate household, institution, responder, utility, government, and community responsibilities; verify rural, remote, disability, language, and non-digital paths.

## Collective trauma, recovery, and institutional displacement

Reopening is not recovery. Track harm, loss, death, injury, displacement, cultural loss, grief, responder burden, school and workplace disruption, public-space loss, institutional closure, narrative conflict, restitution, memorial, truth, psychosocial support, reconnection, right to remain or return, and long-duration follow-up. Community-led practice does not waive privacy, consent, safeguarding, clinical, legal, or public duties.

## Long-horizon collective resilience

Test whether trusted institutions remain open, civic networks retain inclusive governance, mutual aid can surge without exploitation, public information stays verified and reachable, public space supports belonging, isolated residents remain connected, knowledge and culture persist, essential services remain linked, emergency authority normalizes, displaced institutions are restored or replaced, trauma receives repair, and benefits and burdens are fair across current and future communities.

## Evidence and decision boundary

The registry is deliberately empty. There is no verified Phase 82 household-security chain, adopted institution baseline, access or trust finding, admitted civic network, mutual-aid capacity finding, volunteer allocation, cooperative or ownership decision, admitted information ecosystem, integrity finding, truth classification, content suppression, preparedness finding, emergency activation, collective-trauma finding, closure, restoration, reconstruction, remedy, independent review, receipt, score, rank, Phase 64 advance, or operating-outcome change. Each future action requires real dated evidence and a separate human decision.
