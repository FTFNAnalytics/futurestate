## A nation is not an interest group

Public-engagement systems often flatten Indigenous nations, treaty partners, communities, and rights holders into a broad stakeholder list. That framing can obscure jurisdiction, legal duties, political relationships, collective rights, land and water responsibilities, and nation-defined decision processes. Phase 77 begins by recording the specific people, authority, territory, asserted or established rights, treaty relationship, and applicable procedure. It does not infer these from geography or identity labels.

The convening public authority must identify its own duties and limits. A project proponent, regulator, municipality, federal agency, state or provincial body, and Indigenous government may hold different responsibilities. A meeting hosted by one actor cannot discharge another actor’s duty without an explicit and valid basis.

## Keep each process state separate

Notice means information was provided. Engagement means interaction occurred. Consultation involves a defined duty or process. Accommodation addresses identified effects or rights. Agreement records negotiated terms. Consent is a separate finding whose meaning depends on the governing authority and context.

Phase 77 refuses to collapse this sequence. A signed attendance sheet is not consultation. Consultation is not proof that concerns altered the decision. Accommodation offered is not accommodation accepted. Agreement with one body is not consent from every affected rights holder. Process completion is not legitimacy.

## Respect protocol and knowledge governance

Participation methods should not assume that public meetings, written comments, fixed deadlines, open-data publication, or individual attribution are appropriate. Nations may define who speaks, how knowledge is shared, what information remains confidential or collectively governed, how translation occurs, and how seasonal, ceremonial, land-based, or community processes affect timing.

The public record should state these boundaries without disclosing protected knowledge. It can record that evidence exists, who controls it, how it was considered, what constraints apply, and whether the decision maker met the agreed protocol. Transparency is not permission to publish sensitive cultural, ecological, personal, or security information.

## Follow effects across systems and time

Project boundaries often miss cumulative effects on land, water, mobility, access, harvesting, cultural practice, health, housing, workforce, governance capacity, and future generations. A narrow technical permit can still interact with a broader pattern of authorizations and burdens.

Phase 77 links the consultation record to Phase 76 portfolio and cumulative-burden controls. This does not pre-judge an effect or create a portfolio finding. It ensures that cumulative concerns, alternative boundaries, restoration duties, monitoring conditions, and unresolved disagreements remain traceable.

## Preserve disagreement and remedy

Consensus cannot be presumed from continued participation, a lack of litigation, a negotiated condition, or a final government decision. The record preserves positions, disputed facts, jurisdictional disagreements, rejected alternatives, conditions, dissent, appeals, and available remedies. It also records whether a decision can be stayed, reconsidered, amended, reversed, or retired.

This is essential for adaptive governance. New evidence, an incident, changed conditions, non-compliance, or an unfulfilled accommodation may reopen the mandate even after an initial authorization.

## What remains empty

Phase 77 contains no rights determination, consultation process, accommodation, agreement, consent finding, nation-defined protocol, mandate, appeal, or receipt. The guide defines fields and boundaries only. Jurisdiction-specific legal analysis and direct nation-to-nation process remain necessary before any real record can open.
