## Delivery is not access

A notice can be technically delivered and practically unusable. A web page may fail screen readers, a PDF may be untagged, a meeting may lack captions or interpretation, a venue may be physically inaccessible, a deadline may exclude people who need assistance, and a digital-only process may omit people without devices, connectivity, or confidence. Phase 77 treats discoverability, comprehension, format, language, timing, physical access, digital access, and support as separate obligations.

The record captures the intended audiences, the channels used, tests performed, known gaps, requested accommodations, completed accommodations, and unresolved barriers. A generic accessibility statement is not evidence that a particular process was accessible.

## Service users are not one constituency

Current users, prospective users, people denied or unable to use a service, caregivers, nearby non-users, operators, and accessibility advocates may observe different parts of the system. Ridership, enrolment, account, or trip data can miss failed attempts, abandoned journeys, inaccessible interfaces, service refusals, geographic gaps, and people who never enter the measured population.

Phase 77 therefore records both use and exclusion. It asks who is absent from the denominator, what assistance is required, how complaints are captured, and whether a proposed change shifts burden to caregivers, families, public agencies, or alternative services.

## Make participation multimodal

No single channel is universally accessible. Written, phone, video, in-person, asynchronous, facilitated, proxy, and community-hosted methods each create different benefits and barriers. The participation plan explains why the chosen combination fits the affected publics and how equivalent opportunity is provided.

Support may include interpretation, plain-language materials, large print, accessible digital formats, transportation, attendant access, childcare, scheduling, connectivity, compensation, or additional time. The public record can disclose completion and effectiveness without exposing private medical or personal information.

## Separate representation from user evidence

Organizations and advocates can identify systemic barriers, legal duties, design practices, and recurring complaints. Individual users can describe actual service encounters. Administrative data can show scale and distribution. Technical tests can assess conformance. None is a complete substitute for the others.

The issue matrix preserves the type and limit of each contribution. A high comment count does not outweigh an accessibility right, and one favorable experience does not erase a systematic exclusion. A decision maker must respond to material barriers with evidence, conditions, remedy, or an explicit unresolved rationale.

## Accessibility continues after the decision

A mandate can be reopened when service coverage changes, a new user group is affected, complaints rise, a serious incident occurs, technology changes, a safeguard fails, or promised access is not delivered. Adaptive review also asks whether the participation process itself needs correction before the next decision.

This creates continuity between public voice and operating evidence. It does not guarantee a preferred outcome, but it prevents accessibility from ending when the hearing closes.

## What remains empty

Phase 77 records no accessible notice, accommodation, service-user hearing, complaint set, issue response, mandate condition, appeal, or review trigger. The guide defines a future public contract and does not claim that any current FTFN case has completed an accessible participation process.
