## Controls are contextual claims

A cryptographic migration practice, AI evaluation method, access control, monitoring rule, or procurement requirement has meaning only inside a defined system boundary and threat model. The same control can reduce one risk, shift another, or fail when assets, adversaries, data, users, dependencies, or failure consequences change.

Phase 76 records the context before asking whether the control travels.

## Evaluation is not deployment

Laboratory results, pilot coverage, conformance tests, procurement guidance, authorization, production deployment, cutover, legacy retirement, and operating effect are separate states. A method that detects a failure does not prove the system was corrected. An approved migration plan does not prove cryptographic inventory coverage or retirement of vulnerable assets.

The learning record therefore links evaluation findings to corrective actions, accepted changes, residual exceptions, operating incidents, rollback readiness, and mission or service effects.

## Compare evidence quality

Two controls are not comparable merely because they share a label. Reviewers compare test population, version, environment, adversarial conditions, pass criteria, missingness, exclusions, independence, reproducibility, and post-deployment observation.

A weaker test cannot inherit the assurance of a stronger one. A successful test against one threat model cannot erase untested attack paths or operational failure modes.

## Portability creates new duties

Bounded reuse identifies what remains unchanged, what must be retested, who owns acceptance, how exceptions are governed, when rollback is mandatory, and how obsolete controls and data are retired. The receiving institution creates a new accountable decision; it does not inherit the prior authorization.
