## Several endings are possible

A policy can expire, be renewed, narrowed, paused, reversed, superseded, retired, or decommissioned. These states are not synonyms. Supersession identifies a successor rule or decision; it does not prove that old operations stopped, contracts closed, assets were removed, data was handled, users migrated, or harms remedied.

Phase 76 uses fourteen lifecycle gates and ten decommissioning obligations to make the ending reviewable.

## Define the supersession boundary

The successor decision names which provisions, populations, systems, permissions, duties, and periods it replaces. Anything outside that scope remains governed by the prior instrument until another lawful action changes it.

The record also identifies conflicts between old and new rules, transition exceptions, grandfathered cases, dual-running periods, and the authority responsible for resolving ambiguity.

## Decommission operations, not just documents

Retirement can require service migration, user notification, accessibility protection, contract closure, licence changes, asset disposal, technical shutdown, data retention or deletion, workforce transition, environmental restoration, and continuing support. Each obligation has an owner, evidence requirement, due date, exception path, and receipt.

A policy marked obsolete in a document repository may still be active in software, procurement templates, field procedures, contracts, permits, or institutional habits.

## Residual duties survive

Remediation, compensation, restoration, incident investigation, audit, record retention, privacy, security, and legal duties can continue after the policy ends. A retirement decision cannot erase affected parties, prior dissent, unresolved complaints, or harms still requiring action.

The permanent archive preserves the original decision and all later amendments, evidence, challenges, corrections, reversals, and retirement receipts.
