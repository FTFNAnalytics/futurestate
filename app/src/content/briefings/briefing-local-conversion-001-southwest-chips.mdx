## The conversion question

The corridor no longer needs another announcement ledger. It needs a facility-by-facility account of whether announced and partially built capacity can receive electricity and water, staff its lines, install and qualify equipment, and sustain accepted production.

The current record is strongest at the upstream and physical-build stages. Phoenix records a production claim for Fab 1, construction completion for Fab 2, and a topping-out milestone for Fab 3. APS identifies TSMC within its territory; SRP records large-load service conditions and regional grid actions. Phoenix and TSMC have a wastewater agreement, and the corridor has named apprenticeship and workforce-coordination programs.

## What remains open

Those records do not form one completed operating chain. Utility territory is not an executed service agreement. A tariff is not available project capacity. A wastewater agreement is not an accepted treatment asset or measured reuse result. Enrollment capacity is not program completion, placement, or retention. Topping out is not occupancy, process qualification, customer acceptance, or volume production.

The canonical next record is therefore not a larger investment number. It is a source-explicit downstream artifact tied to a named facility: accepted utility infrastructure, metered service, a completion or occupancy record, equipment qualification, accepted product, or compatible production evidence.

## Reader use

Read the corridor as a conversion stack: **buildings → utilities → workforce → qualification → accepted output**. Progress at one stage should update that stage and its next gate without silently advancing the rest of the chain.
