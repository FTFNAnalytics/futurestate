## The conversion question

The Space Coast has real operating infrastructure, but new plans and projects still require a disciplined chain from environmental and site decisions to capital delivery, operator authorization, service, mission use, and measured performance.

The current record includes Kennedy's multi-user master plan, a displayed LC-46 site-license term, final environmental decisions for SLC-40 and LC-39A, the completed Part 450 operator transition, an operational causeway bridge, and one active-countdown land-management operation. NASA's SIMO record exposes the service layer beneath missions but remains a planned acquisition. The Shuttle Landing Facility record still needs a formal current-status artifact.

## What remains open

A master plan is not a funded portfolio. A site license is not authorization for each mission. An environmental decision is not construction or vehicle licensing. One completed asset and one operating event do not establish corridor-wide reliability, utilization, safety, resilience, or local benefit.

The decisive next records are the licence disposition, project awards and agreements, construction and acceptance, operator-specific authorizations, service performance, mission and range utilization, delays, safety events, utility capacity, workforce outcomes, and local effects.

## Reader use

Read the corridor as **plan → environmental decision → site and operator authority → capital delivery → service → mission → repeat performance**. Each record belongs to one stage and should retain its own authority and stop rule.
