## Expertise has a domain

Technical expertise is powerful because it is specialized, methodical, and revisable. It is also bounded. A safety engineer, hydrologist, grid planner, accessibility specialist, operator, economist, community monitor, regulator, and service user may each observe a different part of the same system. Phase 77 records the question each contribution can answer, its method, evidence base, assumptions, uncertainty, conflicts, and limits.

Credentials alone do not determine evidentiary weight. A qualified expert working outside their domain, relying on an incompatible dataset, or serving an undisclosed interest does not acquire authority through title. Conversely, local and experiential knowledge is not dismissed merely because it is not expressed in a formal technical model.

## Separate expertise from decision authority

An expert can assess a hazard, model a scenario, interpret a standard, evaluate a design, or identify uncertainty. That does not automatically authorize a project, allocate a burden, resolve a rights question, or create a public mandate. Decision makers must state the authority they exercise and the public reasons that connect evidence to the choice.

Phase 77 preserves this division. Expert recommendations, agency positions, proponent submissions, community evidence, and final reasons remain distinct records. The final decision cannot cite “the science” as a single undifferentiated conclusion when material disagreement or value choices remain.

## Build a common evidence record

Meaningful deliberation requires a record that participants can discover, understand, test, correct, and supplement. It includes provenance, version, methods, definitions, denominators, exclusions, missingness, uncertainty, conflicts, corrections, and access restrictions. Plain-language explanation sits alongside—not in place of—the underlying technical material.

Protected, proprietary, personal, security-sensitive, or Indigenous-controlled information may require bounded review. The public record can explain what was withheld, why, who reviewed it, and how its limits affected the decision without disclosing the protected content.

## Adjudicate disagreement without manufacturing consensus

Experts can disagree because they use different questions, models, thresholds, values, baselines, evidence, or risk tolerances. Phase 77 asks the response docket to identify the source of disagreement rather than averaging it away. Where evidence cannot resolve a value or distributional choice, the decision maker owns that choice publicly.

A minority technical rationale remains attached to the record. A later correction, replication, incident, or changed condition can reopen the issue. This keeps the process capable of learning without pretending that uncertainty makes every position equally supported.

## Measure influence, not speaking time

Participation quality is not the number of experts invited or minutes allocated. The record asks whether material evidence was accessible, contrary evidence was considered, conflicts were disclosed, questions could be tested, corrections were incorporated, and reasons explain the decision’s evidentiary boundary.

An expert submission that changes a safeguard, baseline, condition, monitoring rule, or review trigger should be traceable. When it does not change the decision, the response should explain why.

## What remains empty

Phase 77 contains no expert panel, common evidence record, conflict review, technical disagreement, reasoned response, legitimacy audit, mandate, or adaptive review. It establishes a future method only and does not elevate any current source or signal into public authorization.
