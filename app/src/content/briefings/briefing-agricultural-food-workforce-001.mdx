A resilient food workforce requires fair pay, safety, housing, transport, migration protection, voice, training, rest, succession, and closure support.

## Evidence architecture

The Phase 84 registry follows one bounded chain: verified collective resilience; production, land, water, and sovereignty; processing, storage, distribution, and procurement; household and institutional access, affordability, and nutrition; then usable reserves, contamination control, circular flows, and long-horizon resource security. Each transition needs named authority, affected-public standing, complete denominators, distribution review, independent challenge, human decision, and dated receipt.

## Production, land, water, and sovereignty

Record production systems by owner, location, land tenure, soil, water, energy, inputs, biodiversity, season, workforce, output, loss, and destination. Local acreage or yield does not prove that food is safe, nutritious, affordable, culturally appropriate, locally retained, or resilient. Indigenous food sovereignty is a jurisdiction and rights question, not a supplier-diversity label; preserve title, treaty, customary harvesting, governance, consent, knowledge, data, benefit, and withdrawal boundaries.

## Processing, storage, logistics, markets, and procurement

Nominal facility or warehouse capacity is not usable capacity. Verify equipment, skilled workers, inspection, water, power, refrigeration, maintenance, packaging, traceability, throughput, condition, contracts, access, alternate routes, and recovery time. Public procurement spend does not prove community benefit: identify awards, ownership, price, margins, jobs, worker safeguards, supplier diversity, retained value, nutritional quality, environmental terms, performance, challenges, and remedies.

## Access, affordability, nutrition, and dignity

Food availability is not nutritional access. Test the full trip from income or eligibility through price, enrollment, hours, travel or delivery, queue, stock, choice, disability and language access, dietary accommodation, safe storage, cooking water and energy, preparation time, consumption, health, and return. Count denials, substitutions, stigma, privacy loss, debt, spoilage, and unmet need. Do not score households or rank communities.

## Institutional meals and community provisioning

School, childcare, hospital, care, shelter, correctional, and other institutional meals create public obligations. Verify portions, nutrition, safety, dietary needs, dignity, continuity, staff, facilities, procurement, waste, complaints, appeals, and independent review. Community kitchens, gardens, fridges, food hubs, mutual aid, and cooperatives can add capacity but cannot erase state duties or substitute unprotected volunteer labor for paid work.

## Reserves, contamination, and circular flows

Inventory is not a usable reserve. Verify ownership, authority, purpose, location, condition, nutritional composition, cultural fit, rotation, expiry, storage, security, release triggers, fair allocation, last mile, replenishment, and audit. Contamination response requires traceability, testing, accessible warning, stop and recall authority, alternate supply, worker and public protection, disposal, restitution, correction, and recurrence prevention. Waste diversion is not circular capacity unless material, nutrition, value, hazards, labor, energy, emissions, and destination are measured without shifting harm.

## Long-horizon community resource security

Test whether land, soil, water, biodiversity, skills, facilities, power, transport, markets, public institutions, community networks, and household capabilities survive climate stress, price shocks, contamination, conflict, closure, and supply disruption. Durable security requires diverse and repairable systems, democratic emergency authority, fair burdens and benefits, restoration, learning, and intergenerational stewardship.

## Decision boundary

This registry is deliberately empty. It contains no verified Phase 83 predecessor, adopted production baseline, land or water allocation, sovereignty finding, processing or storage capacity finding, procurement award, community-benefit finding, inventory allocation, food-access or nutrition finding, eligibility or benefit decision, meal outcome, reserve release, rationing, recall, contamination remedy, circular-capacity finding, resource-security conclusion, review, receipt, score, rank, Phase 64 advance, or operating-outcome change.
