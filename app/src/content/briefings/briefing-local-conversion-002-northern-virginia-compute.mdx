## The conversion question

The central question is no longer whether Northern Virginia hosts a major data-center cluster. It is whether each additional load can be served under a traceable rate, transmission, land, permit, water, and compliance chain without confusing forecast demand with delivered capacity.

The current record provides a strong regional baseline: concentrated data-center load, a PJM forecast that separates firm and non-firm adjustments, Virginia's GS-5 rate class, a conditional Golden-Mars transmission decision, a second named transmission application, a public air-permit trail, and generator-control guidance. Loudoun's active standards process adds a local land-use gate.

## What remains open

No single regional forecast establishes spare capacity or a customer commitment. A rate class is not an executed contract. A transmission application or conditional approval is not a completed and energized asset. An air permit is not installed equipment, a passed test, compliant operation, or an operating data center. A reclaimed-water program is not a facility allocation.

The next evidence should be project-specific and downstream: final orders and routes, land acquisition, construction and inspection records, energization, service agreements, metered load and water, permit tests, operating compliance, reliability, cost, noise, emissions, and local response.

## Reader use

Read this corridor as **forecast → contract → grid build → permits → service → measured effects**. A change at one stage should never be used to advance the later stages automatically.
