## Why challenge is part of publication

A public synthesis should expose a structured way to contest its exact claim, evidence set, methods, assumptions, omissions, and language. Phase 74 creates one challenge-response docket for each named synthesis file, but none is open because no synthesis claim exists.

Challenge is not a comment counter and identity is not a proxy for correctness. The system records standing, expertise, conflicts, funding, evidence, reproducible critique, response, independent adjudication, revision, reader notice, and receipt.

## Twelve external-challenge gates

The challenge packet identifies who submitted it and when, the exact claim version, its bounded scope, every cited artifact, reproducible calculations, method and assumption critiques, omitted or adverse evidence, and the requested disposition. The publication team responds point by point before an independent adjudicator chooses revision, correction, withdrawal, or no material change.

Anonymous assertion is not automatically evidence. Institutional standing does not automatically defeat a well-supported critique. Silent disposition and automatic claim reversal are both prohibited.

## Evidence does not choose the decision

A synthesis addresses what the evidence supports. A decision also depends on authority, objectives, alternatives, costs, feasibility, risks, reversibility, distribution, implementation capacity, and values. Treating an evidence grade as a recommendation hides those judgments.

All eight translation registers remain **Inactive - No Adjudicated Synthesis**. No decision audience, authority, option, benefit, harm, recommendation, monitoring plan, sunset date, reviewer, or receipt exists.

## Fourteen decision-translation gates

Translation begins with the exact decision-maker, authority, and question. Reviewers bind the current synthesis and grade; enumerate action options and the status quo; assess benefits, harms, distribution, equity, feasibility, capacity, cost, dependencies, uncertainty, scenarios, reversibility, safeguards, legal authority, dissent, monitoring, sunset, reevaluation, and dual review.

A recommendation can remain contested even when reviewers agree on the evidence. Recommendation conflicts are first-class records, not drafting noise.

## Options, harms, and distribution

The desk never frames one favored action against an empty alternative. Every option, including waiting or preserving the status quo, receives the same evidence, harm, distribution, feasibility, and uncertainty treatment.

Average benefit cannot hide concentrated burden. A favorable body-level result does not settle who pays, who benefits, which communities carry risk, or whether the receiving institution can implement the option safely.

## Sunset and reevaluation

Ten triggers cover new results, replication, contradiction, adverse evidence, correction, measure changes, context changes, upheld challenges, authority changes, and scheduled sunset. A trigger starts a new human review; it does not silently rewrite the synthesis or extend a recommendation.

Corrections propagate backward to the affected result lineage and forward to synthesis, challenge responses, option records, recommendations, monitoring, and reader notices.

## Decision boundary

The [Evidence Synthesis And Decision Registry](/evidence/synthesis/) is not a policy engine. It creates no recommendation, authorization, adoption, score, rank, forecast, or operating-outcome claim. Human authority and complete receipts remain mandatory.

## Phase 75 accountability, implementation, and impact audit

The [Decision Accountability Desk](/briefings/decision-accountability-desk-001/), [Implementation Commitment Ledger](/briefings/implementation-commitment-ledger-001/), [Realized Impact Audit](/briefings/realized-impact-audit-001/), and [Reversal And Remediation Desk](/briefings/reversal-remediation-desk-001/) add eight inactive accountability dossiers, thirty-two inactive commitment-and-realization ledgers, and eight inactive audit/remediation registers. They create zero authorized decisions, owners, rationales, commitments, baselines, safeguards, outputs, benefits, harms, distributional findings, audits, reversals, remediations, receipts, scores, rankings, or stage changes.
