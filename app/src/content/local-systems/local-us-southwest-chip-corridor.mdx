## System Context

The U.S. Southwest chip corridor is a receiving system where global industrial strategy becomes local demand for water, power, labor, land, suppliers, housing, public incentives, and public legitimacy.

The current source base now spans national semiconductor policy, Arizona electricity and water governance, named utility and provider records, a completed Meta electric-service project, Project Baccara's generation CEC, conditional county permit, and proposed air permit, TSMC campus planning and wastewater agreements, current fab milestones, and active apprenticeship cohorts. It still does not support a corridor-wide readiness or sufficiency conclusion.

## Transduction Question

When a funding announcement, fab milestone, grid constraint, or water policy changes, what outcomes shift locally and which actors have the authority to respond?

## Key Constraints

### Water

Semiconductor manufacturing can require complex water planning, treatment, reuse, provider coordination, and public legitimacy. Arizona Department of Water Resources sources can frame the state water-governance environment, but facility-level water demand and provider-level capacity still need direct evidence.

### Power

Fabs and supplier clusters can intensify electricity demand and reliability needs. EIA sources can provide national and Arizona electricity baselines, while local conclusions still require utility planning, service-territory, interconnection, rate-case, and project documents.

### Labor

Fab construction, operations, suppliers, equipment maintenance, and advanced manufacturing all require specialized workers. This profile now includes a named TSMC apprenticeship and active-cohort counts, but not completion, retention, occupation-level supply, wage, vacancy, migration, or construction-labor evidence.

### Manufacturing

Domestic semiconductor strategy depends on whether funded projects become built, equipped, staffed, qualified, and operational. A City-relayed TSMC update now records a first-fab production claim and second-fab construction completion, but it is not audited output, permit, occupancy, or total-capacity evidence.

### Supply Chain

Supplier clustering can reduce friction only if materials, equipment, logistics, services, and talent co-locate at the right maturity. The current profile does not yet include supplier-network evidence.

### Public Trust

Water stress, public incentives, land use, housing pressure, and energy demand can become legitimacy constraints. The profile needs local public records and community context before making stronger claims about acceptance or opposition.

## Actors With Authority

- Federal agencies shape semiconductor incentives, program requirements, standards, and national industrial strategy.
- State governments shape incentives, workforce programs, water policy, and economic development priorities.
- Municipal governments and local authorities shape land use, permitting, infrastructure coordination, and community response.
- Utilities and water authorities shape service feasibility, reliability, rate structures, and resource planning.
- Semiconductor firms and suppliers decide project timing, facility design, workforce needs, and operational commitments.

## Likely Second-Order Effects

- Fab investment may increase demand for power, water infrastructure, housing, construction labor, and supplier services.
- Utility constraints may alter project sequencing, cost, or location.
- Water constraints may increase the value of reuse, treatment, provider coordination, and public scrutiny.
- Public incentives may attract supplier clustering while increasing political demand for measurable local benefits.
- Workforce shortages may shift hiring, training, wages, and regional migration patterns.

## Phase 51B Service, Planning, And Workforce Records

The dossier now has its first named large-load tariff, industrial wastewater agreement, Phoenix planning case, and facility-linked workforce program.

SRP's E-67 price plan applies to new accounts at or forecast to reach 20 MW and defines minimum-billing, forecast, maximum-load, service-agreement, and possible customer-funded infrastructure conditions. This clarifies the large-load service framework, but the tariff does not name a semiconductor project or prove that facilities of adequate capacity exist at a site.

Phoenix's May 2026 development-agreement record with TSMC is the first project-specific industrial wastewater evidence in the dossier. It identifies lift-station and sewer improvements, a conditional increase in accepted wastewater flow, industrial reclaimed-water-plant triggers, TSMC cost responsibility, and a June 30, 2028 operating deadline for the initial reclaimed-water plant. It does not provide a complete facility water balance, prove construction completion, or establish long-term water sufficiency.

City case `Z-37-20-1` adds the adopted North Phoenix 3,500 PUD and two approved minor amendments as a planning envelope for the campus area. The PUD is not a building-permit, inspection, utility-connection, or occupancy record.

The TSMC registered technician apprenticeship adds named facilities, equipment, process, and manufacturing technician pathways, education partners, company funding, cohort timing, and a 2025 recruitment target. It does not yet report completions, retention, placement, or workforce sufficiency.

The next dossier step is to follow these records downstream: named customer service agreements or energization, completed wastewater and reclaimed-water infrastructure, specific site and building permits, apprenticeship outcomes, construction-labor evidence, and supplier operating records.

## Phase 51C Downstream Service And Facility Evidence

SRP Project Huckleberry supplies the first named customer-service trail. SRP identifies Meta's Mesa Data Center, records initial 69 kV service for a December 2022 in-service date, says Meta funded the beneficiary infrastructure, records the Prickly Pear Substation and 230 kV line in service in May 2024, and labels the project online. This is evidence for one Meta project, not TSMC service or regional spare capacity.

The City of Phoenix's July 2026 TSMC update says Fab 1 has been in N4 volume production since late 2024, Fab 2 construction is complete, and the Arizona operation employs more than 3,500 people. Because the City is relaying company claims, the record does not substitute for audited output, a permit inventory, certificates of occupancy, or utility and water evidence.

Phoenix's November 2025 semiconductor apprenticeship agenda records eight people in the first Facilities Technician cohort and 46 people in a second cohort across process, manufacturing, and facilities roles. This advances the program from target to deployment, but no completion, credential, retention, or placement outcome is reported.

The downstream search deliberately left several lanes open. The dossier still needs completed wastewater and reclaimed-water infrastructure, TSMC-specific power records, named Phoenix permits and occupancy, apprenticeship outcomes, construction-labor evidence, and supplier operating records.

## Phase 55B Facility Power, Water, And Permit Stack

The ACC's February 2026 Project Baccara summary adds a second named large-compute facility trail at a different maturity stage from Project Huckleberry. The Commission approved a CEC for proposed approximately 700 MW on-site natural-gas generation and interconnection, while explicitly distinguishing that decision from approval of the data center itself.

The same official summary relays the developer's plans for chilled cooling and brewery-wastewater reuse. Those statements identify a facility-level water strategy, but they are not built infrastructure or measured performance. The record says Maricopa County and Luke Air Force Base approvals remained pending, and a separate ACC statement identifies an ADEQ air permit and county construction permit among the remaining gates.

The remaining evidence is therefore unusually clear: the full CEC conditions, final Maricopa County air permit and EPA review, fully executed county and military-compliance records, construction and interconnection milestones, and measured power, water, emissions, and operating results. Until those records exist, the dossier treats Project Baccara as a regulatory and planned-infrastructure trail rather than operating capacity.

## Phase 55C Conditional County And Air-Permit Follow-Through

The county trail has now moved beyond the ACC's February snapshot. KJZZ reported a 4-1 Board of Supervisors vote on May 6, 2026 to issue the military compatibility permit. Maricopa County's official agenda for `MCP250007` defines the conditional conversion path: a service agreement or provider will-serve letter, a precise Plan of Development before construction permits, fire review, military-compatibility evidence coordinated with Luke Air Force Base, air-quality permits, final engineering, water-system approvals, and later building and occupancy steps.

The Maricopa County Air Quality Department also issued a notice proposing Permit `P0013417` for the project's power plant and completed a public-comment window. The notice does not establish final issuance, EPA non-objection, construction authorization, or operating emissions compliance.

This advances the project from one state certificate into a conditional county and proposed air-permit stack. It does not close `gap-001` or `gap-002`: service commitments, final permits, built interconnection and reuse infrastructure, measured demand, commissioning, occupancy, and operating performance remain open.
