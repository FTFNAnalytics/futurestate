# Cross-Corridor Infrastructure Conversion Evidence, 2024-2026

Eighteen reviewed official records tracing how compute and grid growth in Northern Virginia, lithium projects in Nevada, and launch infrastructure on Florida's Space Coast move from planning and authorization toward finance, construction, acceptance, operation, and measured outcomes.

Captured: 2026-07-24

## Interpretation boundary

The collection compares evidence stages, not equivalent technologies or markets. A plan is not an appropriation; a permit or site license is not construction or operation; a financial decision is not financial close unless the record says so; financial close is not commissioned capacity; and an analyzed or projected activity level is not measured output, utilization, safety, or local benefit.

## 1. Data Centers in Virginia

**Publisher:** Virginia Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission

**Document type:** Oversight Report

**Capture status:** Original file captured

Virginia's legislative evaluation describes the state's data-center market, its concentration in Northern Virginia, and the electricity, land, water, tax, and community systems exposed to continued growth.

### Key findings

- The report identifies Northern Virginia as the country's largest data-center market and concentrates most estimated state capacity in Loudoun, Prince William, and Fairfax counties.
- It evaluates electricity demand, infrastructure costs, reliability, local land use, water, noise, emissions, and economic-development effects as connected policy questions.
- Its capacity estimates and forecasts are dated baselines rather than a current project, load, or service inventory.

### Why it matters

The report establishes the regional problem definition against which named utility, transmission, permit, water, and local-government records can be tested.

### Evidence limits

- The report does not establish current capacity for a named customer or project.
- Forecast demand is not committed load, completed infrastructure, or delivered service.
- Regional estimates should not be presented as current project inventory without later utility and local records.

**Official source:** https://jlarc.virginia.gov/landing-2024-data-centers-in-virginia.asp

## 3. Virginia SCC GS-5 Large-Load Rate Decision

**Publisher:** Virginia State Corporation Commission

**Document type:** Regulatory Decision

**Capture status:** Official link record

The Virginia SCC approved Dominion Energy Virginia's GS-5 class for future customers at or above 25 MW, with a January 1, 2027 effective date and minimum billing provisions intended to allocate large-load investment risk.

### Key findings

- The decision creates a separate rate class for customers with demand of at least 25 MW.
- The SCC described minimum billing commitments of 85 percent for distribution and transmission and 60 percent for generation.
- The regulatory decision and future effective date precede customer contracts, facilities, energization, billing, and reliability outcomes.

### Why it matters

The record makes customer commitment and cost allocation explicit conversion gates alongside physical power capacity.

### Evidence limits

- The decision does not establish that any named customer has signed under GS-5.
- It does not prove transmission, generation, or distribution capacity is available.
- The future rate design is not evidence of realized bills, load, reliability, or project economics.

**Document type:** Agency Announcement

**Capture status:** Official link record

### Key findings

- The project crossed financial close for a named federal loan rather than remaining at conditional-commitment stage.
