# Thin-Topic Authority Foundations, 2023-2026

Eight reviewed primary records that establish bounded evidence foundations for AI-enabled manufacturing, critical-infrastructure AI governance, agricultural outlooks, post-quantum implementation, cybersecurity baselines, and Phoenix industrial water management.

Captured: 2026-07-24

## Interpretation boundary

A document is included because it supplies a stable primary record for a thin FTFN topic. Research roadmaps are not deployments; concept notes and preliminary drafts are not final requirements; voluntary frameworks are not compliance evidence; forecasts are not observed outcomes; and area-wide water rules are not project water allocations, service commitments, or operating results.

## 1. 2026 Roadmap on Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning for Smart Manufacturing

**Publisher:** IOP Publishing with NIST participation

**Document type:** Research Roadmap

**Capture status:** Official link record

An 83-page peer-reviewed roadmap surveys AI and machine-learning foundations, application areas, and emerging techniques for smart manufacturing while identifying persistent data, integration, reliability, interpretability, and scale barriers.

### Key findings

- The roadmap organizes the field around foundations and trends, application domains such as sensing, autonomous systems, additive manufacturing, digital twins, robotics, supply chains, and sustainability, and emerging methods such as physics-informed, generative, semantic, and foundation-model approaches.
- It identifies noisy or incomplete industrial data, heterogeneous sensing and control systems, limited interpretability, domain shift, real-time performance, and multi-factory scalability as continuing deployment constraints.
- It treats trustworthy operation, reliability, availability, maintainability, safety, and data-centric metrology as necessary components of industrial AI rather than optional overlays.

### Why it matters

The roadmap gives FTFN a current, technically detailed research-priority map for separating promising AI-for-manufacturing methods from the integration and assurance work required for dependable factory use.

### Evidence limits

- The paper is a research roadmap, not a deployment census or product evaluation.
- It does not prove that a named factory has integrated, validated, or scaled the reviewed approaches.
- Forward-looking research directions and expected 2025-2035 developments remain scenarios rather than delivered capabilities.

**Official source:** https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=960263

## 2. NIST AI RMF Trustworthy AI in Critical Infrastructure Profile Concept Note

**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology

**Document type:** Agency Strategy

**Capture status:** Official link record

NIST's concept note launches development of an AI Risk Management Framework profile for AI used across critical-infrastructure IT, operational technology, and industrial control systems.

### Key findings

- The proposed profile is intended to translate AI RMF trustworthiness characteristics into risk-management practices for critical-infrastructure operators, developers, and supply-chain participants.
- The note emphasizes deterministic behavior, explainability, graceful degradation, fail-safe operation, adversarial robustness, rigorous testing, evaluation, validation, and verification, and supply-chain visibility.
- Examples include AI-enabled plant monitoring, autonomous cyber response, diagnostic assistants, industrial robots and vehicles, data-center digital twins, and optimization systems with human oversight.

### Why it matters

The concept defines a concrete governance and assurance agenda for AI entering high-consequence physical systems, where generic AI-risk language is insufficient.

### Evidence limits

- This is a concept note for an ongoing profile-development process, not final guidance.
- It does not create a binding requirement or prove adoption by critical-infrastructure operators.
- Illustrative use cases do not establish deployed capability, safety, or performance.

**Official source:** https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2026/04/08/Concept%20Note_%20Development%20of%20the%20NIST%20AI%20RMF%20Trustworthy%20Use%20of%20AI%20in%20Critical%20Infrastructure%20Profile.pdf

## 3. World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates, July 2026

**Publisher:** USDA World Agricultural Outlook Board

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

WASDE 673 updates official U.S. and world supply, use, trade, stocks, and price projections for major agricultural commodities for the July 2026 forecast vintage.

### Key findings

- The July projection lowered 2026/27 world total-grain ending stocks from the June vintage's 780.59 million metric tons to 771.62 million metric tons.
- The July projection lowered 2026/27 U.S. total-grain ending stocks from 74.08 million metric tons to 68.90 million metric tons while increasing projected U.S. total-grain trade from 109.05 to 110.29 million metric tons.
- Within U.S. wheat, July projected 2026/27 ending stocks at 722 million bushels versus 744 million in the June vintage, while holding the projected average farm price at $6.00 per bushel.

### Why it matters

The report provides a current official forecast baseline for agricultural supply and market risk, with the prior-vintage comparison needed to distinguish an updated outlook from an observed outcome.

### Evidence limits

- WASDE figures are estimates and projections that can be revised in later vintages.
- The report does not establish farm-level production, realized prices, or causal attribution.
- Cross-commodity totals do not substitute for commodity- and region-specific analysis.

**Official source:** https://www.usda.gov/oce/commodity/wasde/wasde0726.pdf

## 4. USDA National Crop Progress, July 20, 2026

**Publisher:** USDA National Agricultural Statistics Service

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

The July 20 weekly national Crop Progress release provides the latest published survey baseline for crop development, condition, fieldwork, and selected weather context before the July 27 release.

### Key findings

- USDA's 2026 release calendar records the July 20 Crop Progress report as published and the July 27 report as pending at the July 24 capture date.
- The report series tracks crop planting, emergence, development, harvest, and condition by crop and state, with current-year, prior-year, and five-year-average comparison structures.
- The weekly record is a time-stamped survey baseline rather than a final yield, price, or farm-level outcome.

### Why it matters

A dated weekly baseline gives FTFN a disciplined entry point for agriculture-and-climate monitoring while preventing a future scheduled release from pausing the broader content program.

### Evidence limits

- The report is survey-based, aggregate, and subject to weekly revision.
- It does not prove farm-level causes, final yield, realized price, or commercial outcome.
- A subsequent weekly report may supersede the latest condition and progress snapshot.

**Official source:** https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Calendar/reports_by_date.php?month=07&view=l&year=2026

## 5. NIST SP 1800-38C: Quantum Readiness Interoperability and Performance Preliminary Draft

**Publisher:** National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence, NIST

**Document type:** Standards and Testbed Record

**Capture status:** Official link record

The preliminary practice-guide volume documents controlled, non-production interoperability and performance testing for draft post-quantum implementations across selected protocols and collaborator components.

### Key findings

- The draft frames interoperability testing as a way to identify and resolve compatibility issues before organizations repeat the same work independently.
- The guide records protocol-specific test profiles, collaborator components, algorithm selections, and performance considerations rather than treating post-quantum migration as a single software update.
- For the documented QUIC test, only one implementation was available at initial testing, so the team could not complete interoperability testing for that protocol.

### Why it matters

The draft supplies inspectable laboratory evidence beneath broad post-quantum policy, while showing that implementation diversity and protocol maturity are practical migration constraints.

### Evidence limits

- The document is a preliminary draft whose public-comment period has closed.
- Controlled laboratory testing does not prove production interoperability, vendor support, procurement, or completed migration.
- Protocol-specific results and implementation availability may be superseded by later standards and software.

**Official source:** https://www.nccoe.nist.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/pqc-migration-nist-sp-1800-38c-preliminary-draft.pdf

## 6. Phoenix Active Management Area Fifth Management Plan

**Publisher:** Arizona Department of Water Resources

**Document type:** Regulatory Decision

**Capture status:** Official link record

The Phoenix AMA Fifth Management Plan establishes conservation, monitoring, reporting, variance, audit, and compliance requirements for municipal, agricultural, and industrial groundwater users in the fifth management period.

### Key findings

- ADWR states that the fifth plans were adopted in 2022 and that their conservation requirements became effective January 1, 2025, remaining in effect until the legislature determines otherwise.
- The plan includes industrial programs for large-scale power plants, large-scale cooling facilities, mining, and new large industrial users, alongside general monitoring and reporting requirements.
- Implementation provisions cover compliance dates, water measurement, records, annual reports, audits, inspections, variances, administrative review, and plan modifications.

### Why it matters

The plan converts broad Arizona water constraints into inspectable sector and compliance requirements relevant to industrial growth in the Phoenix region.

### Evidence limits

- Area-wide conservation rules do not prove a named project's water right, allocation, utility capacity, service agreement, actual use, compliance, or reclaimed-water infrastructure.
- The plan cannot substitute for provider, permit, agreement, facility, or operating records.
- The publication date is represented at year precision because the official landing page provides adoption-year and effective-date context rather than a single document issue date.

**Official source:** https://www.azwater.gov/sites/default/files/media/Phx5MP.pdf

## 7. CISA Cross-Sector Cybersecurity Performance Goals, Version 1.0.1

**Publisher:** Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency

**Document type:** Standards and Testbed Record

**Capture status:** Official link record

CISA's cross-sector performance goals present a voluntary, prioritized baseline of cybersecurity practices intended to reduce common critical-infrastructure risks and map implementation work to NIST Cybersecurity Framework functions.

### Key findings

- CISA selected goals intended to materially reduce common cross-sector risk while remaining actionable and feasible for smaller organizations.
- The revised presentation aligns goals with NIST CSF functions and provides checklist and matrix resources.
- CISA explicitly distinguishes individual goal implementation from complete fulfillment of a referenced NIST CSF subcategory.

### Why it matters

The goals provide a practical baseline that can organize operator evidence requests without confusing voluntary guidance with verified control implementation.

### Evidence limits

- The goals are voluntary and do not independently establish compliance.
- The document does not prove that an organization implemented a control or reduced risk.
- Sector-specific requirements and binding directives must be evaluated separately.

**Official source:** https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-03/CISA_CPG_REPORT_v1.0.1_FINAL.pdf

## 8. The NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology

**Document type:** Standards and Testbed Record

**Capture status:** Official link record

NIST CSF 2.0 provides a voluntary taxonomy of high-level cybersecurity outcomes for organizations of any size, sector, or maturity, with expanded governance and supply-chain emphasis.

### Key findings

- CSF 2.0 is designed for all organizations rather than only critical-infrastructure operators.
- The update adds stronger governance and supply-chain emphasis and organizes outcomes through six functions, including the new Govern function.
- NIST states that the framework does not prescribe how outcomes must be achieved and instead links users to implementation resources, profiles, and informative references.

### Why it matters

CSF 2.0 supplies a common outcome vocabulary for connecting policy, organizational governance, sector profiles, controls, and implementation evidence.

### Evidence limits

- The framework is voluntary unless another authority makes it binding.
- Use, mapping, or claimed alignment does not prove implementation effectiveness or reduced risk.
- The high-level outcome taxonomy does not replace sector, system, asset, control, or incident evidence.

**Official source:** https://www.nist.gov/publications/nist-cybersecurity-framework-csf-20
