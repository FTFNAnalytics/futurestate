# DARPA and U.S. Government Research Angles, 2025-2026

A reviewed collection of 23 recent DARPA, White House, defense, intelligence, energy, and congressional documents that reveal how federal research priorities connect frontier technology to industrial capacity, infrastructure, supply chains, resilience, and national security.

Captured: 2026-07-23

## Interpretation boundary

Summaries distinguish stated priorities from funded execution, program awards, deployment, and local outcomes. Broad solicitations and strategy documents are treated as direction-setting evidence, not proof that a capability has been built.

## 1. DARPA Fiscal Year 2027 Budget Justification

**Publisher:** Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency

**Document type:** Budget Justification

**Capture status:** Original file captured

DARPA's 360-page FY2027 justification requests $5.039 billion and organizes the portfolio around research sciences, information and communications, tactical and space systems, electronics, biological technology, and a dedicated Making, Maintaining, Supply Chain and Logistics program element. It is the strongest document in the collection for seeing where research ambition is tied to budget structure.

### Key findings

- The FY2027 request totals $5.039 billion in discretionary RDT&E.
- Making, Maintaining, Supply Chain and Logistics appears as a dedicated program element alongside information, electronics, biomedical, tactical, space, sensing, and command-and-control work.
- The portfolio spans basic research through advanced systems, making transition and industrial capacity recurring dependencies rather than downstream afterthoughts.

### Why it matters

The budget book shows that logistics, production, sustainment, and supply-chain resilience are being treated as research objects alongside frontier technical capability.

### Evidence limits

- A budget request is not an enacted appropriation or an award record.
- Program-element funding does not prove technical success, transition, procurement, or deployment.

## 9. FY2027 Administration R&D Budget Priorities (M-25-34 / NSTM-2)

**Publisher:** Office of Management and Budget and Office of Science and Technology Policy

**Document type:** Policy Memorandum

**Capture status:** Original file captured

M-25-34 and NSTM-2 direct agencies to align FY2027 R&D with critical and emerging technologies, energy, national security, health and biotechnology, and space. AI, quantum, semiconductors, advanced communications, advanced manufacturing, critical minerals, and the infrastructure beneath them are treated as a connected federal portfolio.

### Key findings

- AI priorities include new architectures, efficiency, evaluation, security, scientific discovery, autonomy, and structured datasets.
- Quantum, semiconductors, communications, manufacturing, energy, minerals, biotechnology, and space are framed as mutually supporting national capabilities.
- The memorandum links research choices to economic growth, high-wage employment, and national security.

### Why it matters

It is the clearest cross-government statement of which technical and physical systems agencies were told to prioritize in the FY2027 budget cycle.

### Evidence limits

- Budget guidance shapes agency submissions but does not itself appropriate funds.
- The priorities are broad and do not guarantee specific programs, awards, or outcomes.

## 11. 2026 National Defense Strategy

**Document type:** National Strategy

**Capture status:** Official link record

The 2026 National Defense Strategy is organized around four lines of effort: defend the homeland, deter China in the Indo-Pacific, increase allied burden-sharing, and supercharge the defense industrial base. ### Why it matters

It makes industrial capacity and logistics explicit strategic outcomes for the research and technology portfolio.

### Evidence limits

- The strategy does not identify the performance of specific programs or suppliers.

The AI Action Plan lays out more than 90 federal actions under innovation, infrastructure, and international security pillars. It treats AI leadership as a full-stack problem involving models, scientific data, chips, data centers, power, permitting, workforce, cybersecurity, exports, and secure government compute.

**Document type:** Draft Study

**Capture status:** Original file captured

DOE's draft study finds a pressing need for additional transmission as data centers, domestic manufacturing, large industrial loads, electrification, and new generation increase demand. It identifies within-region and interregional congestion patterns but does not prescribe specific projects.

### Key findings

## 23. CRS Defense Primer: Emerging Technologies

**Publisher:** Congressional Research Service

**Document type:** Congressional Primer

**Capture status:** Original file captured

The CRS primer synthesizes the defense implications of artificial intelligence, lethal autonomous systems, hypersonic weapons, directed energy, biotechnology, and quantum technology. ### Key findings

- The technologies differ materially in maturity, operational use, and governance questions.

### Why it matters

The primer provides a nonpartisan synthesis that helps keep technology claims bounded by maturity and oversight context.

### Evidence limits

- The primer is a high-level synthesis and does not replace program-specific technical evidence.
- Fast-moving fields may have changed since the January 2026 update.
