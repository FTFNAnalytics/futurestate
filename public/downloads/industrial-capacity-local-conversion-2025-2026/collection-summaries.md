# Industrial Capacity and Local Conversion Evidence, 2025-2026

Ten reviewed official records connecting national industrial-capacity programs and datasets to the local labor, infrastructure, procurement, permitting, and regulatory evidence needed to assess conversion into delivered capacity.

Captured: 2026-07-24

## Interpretation boundary

Program selection is not an award or operating project; an inventory is not a commitment; a permit authorization is not a start or completion; a preliminary capital pipeline is not a solicitation; a regulatory application is not a decision; and a completed environmental review does not prove construction, qualification, production, local service capacity, or economic outcomes.

## 1. Critical Minerals: Selected Technologies for Substitution and Recycling Could Reduce U.S. Supply Chain Risks

**Publisher:** U.S. Government Accountability Office

**Document type:** Oversight Report

**Capture status:** Official link record

GAO-26-108687 assesses substitution and recycling technologies for battery and semiconductor supply chains and distinguishes nearer-term recycling potential from the longer adoption and infrastructure path facing several semiconductor applications.

### Key findings

- GAO reports that recycling could reduce import reliance for some battery materials within roughly two to three years, subject to collection, processing, qualification, cost, and market conditions.
- For several semiconductor materials, substitution and recycling approaches are less likely to reduce near-term import reliance because adoption, qualification, recovery infrastructure, and commercial scale remain limited.
- The report frames supply-chain resilience as a system problem spanning material performance, product design, collection, processing, standards, economics, and manufacturing adoption.

### Why it matters

The report provides a current oversight baseline for separating technical options from the adoption, qualification, infrastructure, and economic evidence required before they change industrial supply risk.

### Evidence limits

- Technology potential and expert time ranges are not forecasts of realized domestic supply.
- The report does not prove that a named project is financed, permitted, operating, qualified, or reducing imports.
- Battery and semiconductor material pathways differ and should not be generalized across commodities or applications.

**Official source:** https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108687

## 2. DOE Selects 19 Critical-Minerals Processing Projects

**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy

**Document type:** Agency Announcement

**Capture status:** Official link record

DOE announced more than $45.7 million for 19 selected projects intended to advance pilot-scale processing and recovery of critical materials, including magnesium and rare-earth elements.

### Key findings

- The announcement identifies 19 selected projects and more than $45.7 million in intended federal support.
- DOE describes the work as pilot-scale processing and recovery development rather than commercial production.
- The selected portfolio includes material-specific projects whose later evidence must be tracked through awards, project execution, facility records, validation, and production.

### Why it matters

The selections move the federal critical-minerals trail one stage downstream from an open funding opportunity, while leaving award execution, technical performance, scale, cost, and production unproven.

### Evidence limits

- A selection announcement is not necessarily a completed award or obligation.
- Pilot-scale intent does not establish technical success, commercial economics, permitting, offtake, or production.
- The announcement does not demonstrate reduced import reliance or resilient supply at system scale.

**Official source:** https://www.energy.gov/cmei/articles/does-office-critical-minerals-and-energy-innovation-announces-over-45-million-support

## 3. Form EIA-860 Early Release: 2025 Generator Inventory

**Publisher:** U.S. Energy Information Administration

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

EIA's early-release 2025 Form 860 files provide generator-level records for operable, proposed, retired, canceled, and postponed units, with an explicit preliminary-release boundary.

### Key findings

- The release separates existing, proposed, retired, canceled, and postponed generator records rather than treating them as one capacity total.
- Fields support technology, capacity, status, ownership, location, and planned-date analysis at generator level.
- EIA identifies the release as early data that can be revised before the final annual publication.

### Why it matters

Form 860 gives FTFN a consistent inventory rail for testing power-capacity claims while preserving generator status and release vintage.

### Evidence limits

- A proposed generator record is not a construction commitment or operating asset.
- Early-release values are preliminary and may be revised.
- Generator inventory alone does not prove transmission deliverability, local interconnection, customer service, reliability, or price.

**Official source:** https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia860/

## 4. Form EIA-923 Monthly Power Plant Operations Data Through May 2026

**Publisher:** U.S. Energy Information Administration

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

EIA's July 23 release extends monthly plant-level generation, fuel consumption, receipts, stocks, and selected operating data through May 2026.

### Key findings

- The files provide plant-level monthly observations rather than generator planning status.
- Generation, fuel-use, receipt, cost, quality, and stock fields can be combined only with careful attention to file structure and reporting coverage.
- Monthly and annual products have different release timing and can be revised.

### Why it matters

Form 923 supplies observed plant-operation evidence that complements Form 860's generator inventory and helps prevent planned capacity from being mistaken for production.

### Evidence limits

- Reported plant operations do not prove local service availability for a named industrial customer.
- Monthly data are subject to coverage, revision, aggregation, and confidentiality limitations.
- Plant output alone does not establish transmission headroom, interconnection availability, reliability, emissions compliance, or marginal cost.

**Official source:** https://www.eia.gov/electricity/data/eia923/

## 5. County Employment and Wages in Arizona: Fourth Quarter 2025

**Publisher:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

BLS reports that Maricopa County had 2,318,500 covered jobs in December 2025, down 0.1 percent from a year earlier, while its fourth-quarter average weekly wage was $1,512, up 2.3 percent.

### Key findings

- Maricopa County's December 2025 covered employment was 2,318,500, a 0.1 percent year-over-year decline.
- The county's fourth-quarter 2025 average weekly wage was $1,512, 2.3 percent above the prior-year quarter.
- QCEW covers more than 95 percent of U.S. jobs but remains a lagged administrative dataset with disclosure and industry-detail limits.

### Why it matters

The release adds a current county-level employment and wage baseline to the Phoenix industrial-conversion dossier, complementing metropolitan occupation estimates.

### Evidence limits

- County totals do not isolate semiconductor employers, occupations, vacancies, or skills.
- The data do not prove workforce availability, training fit, retention, productivity, or project readiness.
- Quarterly releases are lagged and detailed values can be suppressed or revised.

## 6. Building Permits Survey: Final Annual 2025 Data

**Publisher:** U.S. Census Bureau

**Document type:** Data Release

**Capture status:** Official link record

The final 2025 Building Permits Survey provides place-, county-, state-, and structure-level residential authorization data for housing-pipeline analysis.

### Key findings

- The annual files report authorized housing units and valuation by permit-issuing geography and structure type.
- The May 14 release is the final annual 2025 vintage and should remain distinct from monthly preliminary estimates.
- Authorization counts are an upstream housing-stage measure rather than starts, completions, occupancy, availability, or affordability.

### Why it matters

Housing authorization is one measurable input to local workforce and industrial-growth analysis, provided it remains separate from delivered housing supply.

### Evidence limits

- Permits authorize construction; they do not prove starts, completions, occupancy, availability, or affordability.
- Coverage, imputation, revisions, and permit-issuing geography require explicit treatment.
- Residential authorization does not directly measure industrial workforce adequacy or project delivery.

**Official source:** https://www.census.gov/construction/bps/annual.html

## 10. CHIPS Program National Environmental Policy Act Implementation

**Publisher:** National Institute of Standards and Technology

**Document type:** Agency Strategy

**Capture status:** Official link record

NIST's CHIPS NEPA page sets out revised environmental-review procedures, applicant information requirements, project review materials, and completed-review records for CHIPS incentives projects.

### Key findings

- NIST published revised CHIPS programmatic NEPA procedures on June 30, 2025.
- The page links applicant forms and project-specific review records, including environmental assessments, findings, environmental impact statements, and records of decision where applicable.
- Review artifacts represent distinct procedural stages and must remain separate from award, permit, construction, production, and local outcome evidence.

### Why it matters

The framework provides an official rail for testing environmental-review claims in semiconductor project timelines without converting a review stage into delivery.

### Evidence limits

- NEPA review does not replace every state, local, or sector permit.
- A completed review does not prove construction, schedule, qualification, production, utility capacity, or economic outcomes.
- Project descriptions and anticipated impacts can change and should be tied to the exact document and decision stage.

**Official source:** https://www.nist.gov/chips/implementation-strategies/national-environmental-policy-act-nepa-and-chips-act
