## What Changed

NIST's final-award notice names four advanced-packaging actions: $100 million each to Absolics, Applied Materials, and Arizona State University, plus $1.1 billion to Natcast for advanced-packaging capabilities at the Tempe prototyping and piloting facility.

Commerce later announced that NIST would assume NSTC operations and stated that a prior agreement granting Natcast up to $7.4 billion was invalid. That action creates a material discontinuity for the Natcast-dependent trail. It does not supply a complete disposition for each award named in the January notice.

## Evidence Stage

This record supports `January final-award notice + named facility and technical scope + later program-governance discontinuity`. It does not support `every award remains active`, `every award canceled`, `facility operational`, `tools installed`, `process qualified`, `customer access`, or `commercial production`.

## Why It Matters

The awards show how the U.S. packaging stack is being assembled across glass-core substrates, silicon-core substrates, wafer and panel-level processing, and a shared pilot facility. Those are separable implementation lanes with different equipment, material, workforce, and commercialization gates.
