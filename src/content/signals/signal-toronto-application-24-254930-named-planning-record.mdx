## What Changed

FTFN selected Toronto planning application `24 254930 ESC 20 OZ` as the first named application record in the Ontario real-estate dossier. The City Clerk's August 29, 2025 public notice identifies the affected properties at 507-513 Victoria Park Avenue and 4, 6 and 14 Thora Avenue.

The notice describes proposed Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendments for two mixed-use buildings of 39 and 16 storeys, with 578 residential units, retail and school or daycare space, park space, amenity space, bicycle parking, and vehicle parking.

## Why It Matters

This record replaces a generic “municipal applications matter” statement with a traceable application number, address, decision body, date, application types, and proposed unit count. It gives FTFN a concrete starting point for following how proposed housing moves—or fails to move—through municipal review and later delivery stages.

## Dependency Stack

- Complete Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendment application
- Planning review and public process
- Municipal decision and any conditions
- Site servicing and infrastructure capacity
- Building-permit applications
- Financing and construction start
- Completion and occupancy

## Evidence Limits

The notice proves that the City received and publicly noticed the planning applications described. It does not prove approval, project viability, servicing capacity, financing, building permits, a start date, completion, occupancy, affordability, or delivery of 578 units.

The Application Information Centre refreshes daily and can change as review proceeds. Because its map was unavailable during the check, this signal preserves only the dated public-notice facts and does not infer the application's current status.

## What To Watch Next

Watch AIC status and document updates, staff reports, community-consultation records, council or community-council decisions, revised unit counts, servicing and infrastructure materials, building permits, starts, and completion or occupancy evidence.
