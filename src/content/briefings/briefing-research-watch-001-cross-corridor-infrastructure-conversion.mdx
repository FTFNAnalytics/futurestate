## The conversion question

Phase 55V asks a narrower question than whether these corridors are important: **what primary record would prove that an upstream decision became usable capacity?**

The [18-document research collection](/research/cross-corridor-infrastructure-conversion-2024-2026/) supplies six official records for each system and a downloadable evidence bundle. The records are intentionally heterogeneous. A legislative evaluation, utility rate decision, mine permit, federal loan decision, master plan, site license, and environmental assessment do different work. The comparison is the stage path each creates.

## Corridor one: compute demand to customer service

Northern Virginia's shelf starts with a dated legislative baseline for concentrated data-center capacity. It then exposes the local transmission-planning process, a separate rate class for future loads of at least 25 MW, a named transmission decision with a local route condition, a facility-level air-permit registry, and a reclaimed-water program.

Together, those records establish that compute growth is not one power number. It is a sequence:

1. demand and location;
2. customer commitment and rate treatment;
3. generation, transmission, distribution, land, and route decisions;
4. construction and environmental compliance;
5. energization and water connection;
6. delivered service, operating use, reliability, cost, and local outcomes.

The next high-value record is not another regional forecast. It is a named project's final route, construction, energization, service agreement, measured load, water use, and reliability result.

## Corridor two: mineral authorization to qualified output

Nevada's shelf follows Thacker Pass and Rhyolite Ridge through federal land decisions, state environmental permits, and federal financing decisions. Thacker Pass has a closed $2.26 billion federal loan; that is materially further along than a policy announcement, but projected output is still not production. Rhyolite Ridge has a BLM land decision and a DOE decision stating an intent to issue a loan for defined facilities; the captured DOE record is not independent evidence of financial close or disbursement.

The next sequence is:

1. authorization and remaining permit conditions;
2. finance close and draws;
3. construction and inspection;
4. commissioning and environmental compliance;
5. product qualification and customer acceptance;
6. sustained output, sales, repayment, and measured impacts.

The collection therefore narrows the Nevada gap. The question is no longer whether the projects have official attention. It is which downstream milestone has a named, current primary record.

## Corridor three: spaceport plan to utilized asset

Florida's shelf starts with Kennedy's multi-user master plan and then moves through an FAA site-license inventory, SLC-40's final environmental decision, the Part 450 transition, FDOT's state capital program, and Space Florida's annual portfolio report.

The delivery sequence is:

1. long-range plan and land envelope;
2. appropriation, state funding, or financing;
3. environmental and site decisions;
4. design, procurement, construction, and acceptance;
5. operator, vehicle, and mission authorization;
6. range availability, launch or reentry, safety, utilization, and economic outcomes.

The analyzed SLC-40 envelope of up to 120 annual launches and 34 booster landings is not achieved cadence. The Launch Complex 46 site term is not a mission approval. Kennedy plan concepts are not appropriated projects. Those boundaries identify the next useful records.

## Editorial boundary

This briefing does not rank the corridors or claim that their legal instruments are interchangeable. It maps record succession. Every later claim should retain a named project, exact authority, date, stage, and the next record needed to prove conversion.
